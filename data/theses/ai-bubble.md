## Why this report exists

Half the internet says AI is the biggest bubble in history; the other half says it's the most important technology ever built. This report started from a widely-shared video — ["The AI Bubble… We Need to Talk" (Casually Finance)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J2Fb1bBufA) — that argues both sides are asking the wrong question. Its claim: stop arguing about *demand* (users, adoption, revenue narratives) and watch *supply* — the capital being committed upfront — because that's what actually decides how booms end.

We fact-checked the video's specific numbers against primary sources, then went wider: the strongest verified evidence **for** the bubble thesis, the strongest evidence **against** it, and what the capital-cycle framework implies for the names this site tracks (NVDA, BTC, SK Hynix, Micron, Nebius, Bloom Energy, CoreWeave).

## The video's argument in 60 seconds

The framework is **capital cycle theory** (popularized by Marathon Asset Management, rooted in Adam Smith): returns are driven by supply, not demand. Every cycle runs four stages:

1. **The flood** — a new technology shows sexy returns; capital pours in.
2. **The boom** — competitors pile in faster than demand grows; returns start slipping.
3. **The collapse** — new supply crushes the returns that attracted it; prices fall, companies fail.
4. **The inheritance** — capital flees, builders go bankrupt, and latecomers buy the assets for pennies. *This* is where the real money gets made.

The video's historical rhymes: UK railway mania (1840s), the fiber-optic buildout (late 1990s — ~$500B buried, most of it dark for years), and US shale (2014–16 oil bust). Its verdict: AI is somewhere between stages two and three, demand is being partly manufactured by circular financing, and the honest question was never "bubble or not" — it's *who floods in, who drowns, and who inherits*.

## Fact-checking the video

We verified the video's core statistics against primary sources, with three independent checks per claim where possible. Scorecard:

| Video claim | Verdict | What we found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Big tech to spend ~$725B on AI this year | ✅ Confirmed | Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta collectively [guided to roughly $725B of 2026 capex](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/magnificent-7-earnings-rush-reveals-ai-spending-surge-with-hyperscaler-capex-set-to-reach-725-billion-in-2026-224901707.html), up ~77% from ~$410B in 2025 — [most of it AI infrastructure](https://www.statista.com/chart/35046/capital-expenditure-of-meta-alphabet-amazon-and-microsoft/). Goldman's supply-side model puts total AI capex at [~$765B in 2026, ~$1.6T by 2031](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01575v1). |
| AI investment = 4% of GDP but 92% of H1 2025 US GDP growth (Furman) | ✅ Confirmed, with a caveat the video skipped | [Furman's own numbers](https://x.com/jasonfurman/status/1971995367202775284): info-processing equipment & software was 4% of GDP, 92% of H1 2025 growth; ex-AI growth was [~0.1% annualized](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/data-centers-gdp-growth-zero-first-half-2025-jason-furman-harvard-economist/). But Furman himself cautioned it's an accounting decomposition, not a counterfactual — absent the boom, lower rates and power prices would have generated offsetting growth, maybe half of it. |
| OpenAI lost $38.5B in 2025; S&M up ~400% to $5.7B (44% of revenue) | ✅ Confirmed, with a big footnote | The [leaked audited financials](https://www.wheresyoured.at/exclusive-openai-financials/) show a $38.53B net loss and S&M of $5.73B (up from $1.11B) against $13.07B revenue. The footnote: [~$41.5B of the loss was a one-time non-cash charge](https://mlq.ai/news/leaked-openai-financials-reveal-21b-operating-loss-on-13b-revenue-in-2025/) from the for-profit conversion. Operating loss was $20.9B; adjusted net loss ~$8B. Ugly, but not $38.5B-of-cash-incinerated ugly. |
| Circular financing: Nvidia → OpenAI → Oracle → back to Nvidia | ✅ The flows are real; the interpretation is contested | Nvidia committed [$100B to OpenAI, most of which flows back as chip purchases](https://fortune.com/2025/09/28/nvidia-openai-circular-financing-ai-bubble/) (per OpenAI's own CFO); Nvidia holds ~$3B of CoreWeave, which [borrowed against Nvidia chips to buy more Nvidia chips](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2025/11/10/nvidia-deals-vendor-financing-round-tripping). Relative to revenue, Nvidia's ~$110B of ecosystem financing is [about 2.8x Lucent's dot-com-era exposure](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/). But see the bull case below — circularity alone is not diagnostic of a bubble. |
| Broadcom lost ~$300B in one session on soft guidance (June 2026) | ✅ Roughly confirmed | Shares fell ~14–15% on June 4, 2026, [on track to erase ~$315B of market value](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/artificial-intelligence/2026/06/04/broadcom-set-to-shed-us300-billion-in-value-as-ai-results-fail-to-impress/). The nuance: AI revenue actually grew 143% y/y and [guidance was reiterated, not cut](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/03/broadcom-avgo-earnings-report-q2-2026.html) — at ~100x trailing earnings, merely-in-line was enough. The sector lost roughly $1.3T that session. |
| 37 of 51 major breakthroughs (1825–2000) triggered bubbles | ⚠️ Couldn't verify | We could not locate this study to confirm the numbers. The qualitative point — great technologies routinely produce financial bubbles (railways, radio, internet) — is well supported by history anyway. |
| Historical analogs: railway mania, ~$500B fiber buildout with 85% dark fiber, shale bust | ⚠️ Directionally right, numbers unverified | The broad histories are accurate (fiber overbuild with massive dark capacity, bandwidth price collapse, 2014–16 oil bust with 200+ bankruptcies). We did not independently verify the specific percentages. Lucent's ~$8B of vendor financing [is documented](https://www.acadian-asset.com/investment-insights/owenomics/straight-talk-about-circular-deals-in-ai); Nortel lent $7B+ to its own customers and fell from ~$398B of market value to 18 cents a share. |

Bottom line on the video: **its numbers largely check out**, its framework is a legitimate one used by real investors, and its two biggest rhetorical moves — using the headline $38.5B OpenAI loss without the one-time-charge caveat, and using Furman's 92% without Furman's own caveat — both overstate an already-strong case.

## The bear case, on the verified evidence

**1. The spend is historically enormous and still accelerating.** ~$725B from four companies in 2026, roughly double 2025. Analysts already project [over $1.1 trillion of combined hyperscaler capex in 2027](https://www.man.com/insights/the-ai-bubble). Estimates of the full data-center buildout run [$3–5 trillion by 2028–29](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/02/03/856623.htm), with McKinsey stretching to [$7T by 2030](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/ai-data-centers-financing-insurance-deals-gpu-debt.html). Capital floods of this size are precisely what the capital cycle says destroys returns.

**2. The US economy is leaning on it.** Furman's math again: without the AI/data-center buildout, H1 2025 GDP growth was ~0.1%. Even halving that dependence per his own caveat, a capex slowdown is no longer just a tech-sector story — it's a macro event.

**3. The funding has shifted from cash to debt — and moved into opaque structures.** This is the strongest *new* evidence, and it's a real change from a year ago: hyperscalers historically funded capex from free cash flow, but Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle [borrowed $93B in investment-grade bonds in 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/02/03/856623.htm) (6% of all US issuance), with $250–300B more expected in 2026. Private-credit exposure to AI already exceeds $200B; the BIS projects $300–600B by 2030 and warns it "could amplify shocks." Meta's ~$27B "Beignet" SPV keeps most of its flagship data-center cost off balance sheet. xAI borrowed at a 12.5% coupon. CoreWeave's debt has grown from under $8B (2024) to [over $21B](https://qz.com/gpu-collateralized-debt-ai-neocloud-coreweave-financing-risks-050526), and GPU-collateralized paper is leaking into ordinary bond funds. Four US senators have [called for an investigation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/ai-data-centers-financing-insurance-deals-gpu-debt.html) into the opacity. When the 1999 comparison was made a year ago, the rebuttal was "this time it's funded by cash flow." That rebuttal is expiring.

**4. Accounting choices are flattering the picture.** Hyperscalers have [extended GPU depreciation schedules to 5.5–6 years](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/) even though heavily-utilized GPUs reportedly last 1–3 years (Meta has seen ~9% annual failure rates). Longer schedules mean lower reported costs today. Meanwhile lenders underwrite GPU collateral whose resale market is thin and whose architecture is [two generations stale before a five-year loan matures](https://qz.com/gpu-collateralized-debt-ai-neocloud-coreweave-financing-risks-050526).

**5. The revenue side is real but still far behind, and the unit economics are treacherous.** Only [11% of companies quantified AI productivity gains on earnings calls; only 2% quantified an earnings impact](https://www.man.com/insights/the-ai-bubble). Token prices fall >70% per year — great for users, brutal for providers: keeping inference revenue merely *flat* requires >225% annual growth in tokens served. And the market is priced for perfection: Broadcom's ~$300B one-day haircut for *reiterating* guidance is what late-cycle expectations fragility looks like.

**6. Concentration risk is at record levels.** The Magnificent 7 are [~34% of the S&P 500](https://www.forbes.com/sites/investor-hub/article/sp-500-weight-mag-7-stocks-diversification-risk/) (the pre-2023 record for top-10 concentration was 28%, set in 1970). Nvidia alone is ~8% of the index. If the AI trade cracks, there is no hiding in "the market" — the AI trade *is* the market.

## The bull case — what the bears skip

**1. The revenue is not imaginary, and it's compounding fast.** Microsoft's AI business hit a [$37B annual run rate growing 123% y/y](https://www.statista.com/chart/35046/capital-expenditure-of-meta-alphabet-amazon-and-microsoft/); Google Cloud grew 60%+ with a backlog that nearly doubled in a quarter to $460B; Nvidia just printed [a record $81.6B quarter](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01575v1). Anthropic went from [$1B to $30B+ of annualized revenue in 15 months](https://www.the-ai-corner.com/p/anthropic-30b-arr-passed-openai-revenue-2026) and projects positive free cash flow by 2027. Even OpenAI's unit economics improved — $1.60 spent per $1 of revenue in 2025, down from $2.37. The dot-com bubble's poster children had no revenue; these have some of the fastest-growing revenue lines in corporate history.

**2. The funders are the most profitable companies on Earth.** Nvidia's top four customers generated [$451B of operating cash flow in 2024](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/); Nvidia itself sits on $46B of net cash (Lucent, at the same point in its cycle, had $304M of quarterly operating cash flow and was later charged with revenue manipulation). Debt is rising, but from a base of enormous internally-generated cash — a categorically different starting point than 1999's telcos.

**3. Circularity is not, by itself, a bubble signal.** Acadian's Owen Lamont — a bubble scholar, not a cheerleader — argues [circular deals are neither necessary nor sufficient for a bubble](https://www.acadian-asset.com/investment-insights/owenomics/straight-talk-about-circular-deals-in-ai): DuPont's 1917 stake in GM was circular and wildly profitable for 40 years. His falsifiable warning signs — a wave of AI IPOs, share-funded acquisition binges, megacaps halting buybacks — were largely absent as of his writing. Watch those, not the arrows-on-a-diagram chart.

**4. Demand still exceeds supply at the frontier.** Management teams describe *under*-building, not overbuilding, as the bigger risk; compute remains rationed; power delivery (not demand) is the binding constraint, with [2.5–3 year lead times on generation equipment](https://www.fabricatedknowledge.com/p/capital-cycles-and-ai). Classic bubbles end in unsold inventory; this one, so far, still has queues.

**5. The most careful academic read is deliberately non-binary.** A 2026 survey of the evidence concludes AI is best understood as [a real technological revolution with localized bubble dynamics](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01575v1) — not a pure mania. That matches every prior transformative buildout: the railways and the fiber were *both* a bubble *and* the backbone of the next fifty years. Both things were true. They can be true again.

## Our verdict

**"Bubble or not" is the wrong resolution — the right question is where the losses concentrate.** The verified picture: real, explosive revenue growth AND a capex flood that has outrun it; cash-rich funders AND a rapid new shift into opaque debt; genuine scarcity today AND classic late-cycle behavior (priced-for-perfection selloffs, depreciation stretching, vendor financing at 2.8x dot-com intensity relative to revenue).

That is exactly what "a revolution with localized bubble dynamics" looks like. The technology succeeding and the average dollar invested in its infrastructure losing money are fully compatible outcomes — that's the railway/fiber lesson, and it's the capital cycle's core claim.

**Signals we're watching** (each falsifiable, each would move our read):

- Hyperscaler capex guidance: the first *cut* (not "growth slowdown") is the cycle-turn signal.
- Lamont's three: an AI IPO wave, share-funded acquisition binges, megacap buyback halts.
- Credit: spreads on GPU-backed/neocloud debt, any SPV or private-credit stress event, further senator/regulator escalation.
- Depreciation: any hyperscaler *shortening* GPU depreciation schedules (an admission that flatters reversed).
- Monetization: the share of companies quantifying AI earnings impact (currently 2%) — this number has to grow into the capex or the capex has to shrink to it.
- Token demand: usage growth sustainably above the ~225%/yr needed to offset price deflation.

## What the capital cycle implies for the names this site tracks

Not advice — this is the framework's logic applied to our coverage list, so you can pressure-test your own sizing.

- **CoreWeave (CRWV) and Nebius (NBIS) — the leveraged supply side.** In capital-cycle terms, neoclouds are the railway builders: they borrow (CRWV: $21B+ of debt, GPU-collateralized, dependent on take-or-pay contracts from a handful of hyperscalers) to build the supply everyone else uses. Highest torque in the boom, first to drown if hyperscalers cut or renegotiate — the collateral and the customer are the same bet. These are the positions the bear case is *about*.
- **NVIDIA (NVDA) — the arms dealer with a loan book.** Still the cycle's greatest beneficiary, with real cash and real margins. But ~$110B of ecosystem financing (~67% of trailing revenue), 39% of revenue from two customers, and its role as lender-of-last-resort to its own buyers means it is no longer a pure picks-and-shovels play — it now shares its customers' downside. Watch its investment commitments the way you'd watch a bank's loan book.
- **Micron (MU) and SK Hynix — classic capital-cycle cyclicals.** Memory is the textbook boom-bust industry: HBM demand is real and supply is tight *now*, but memory makers respond to high prices by adding capacity, and the bust arrives through oversupply. They are simultaneously prime beneficiaries and the historical proof of the framework. Position for a cycle, not a permanent plateau.
- **Bloom Energy (BE) — the power buildout.** Doug O'Laughlin's capital-cycle read predicts [power infrastructure and data centers, not chips, become the overbuilt segment](https://www.fabricatedknowledge.com/p/capital-cycles-and-ai). Power demand is the most durable part of the story (electricity use ~doubling by 2030 per the IEA), but if capex guidance cracks, orders for on-site generation are cancellable. Middle of the risk spectrum.
- **Bitcoin (BTC) — not an AI asset, but not insulated.** BTC trades as a liquidity-sensitive risk asset. An AI-led selloff that hits the Mag 7 (34% of the index) is a risk-off, liquidity-draining event; expect correlation in the drawdown even though the thesis is unrelated. The offset: a bust would likely bring rate cuts, historically a BTC tailwind on the other side.
- **The inheritance play (nobody's ticker yet).** The capital cycle's last lesson: the biggest fortunes are made *after* the collapse, buying built infrastructure — data centers, power capacity, dark GPUs — for pennies. If the cycle turns, the shopping list is the same list as today's casualty list. Keeping dry powder is a position too.
