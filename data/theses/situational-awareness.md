## The thesis in one paragraph

Leopold Aschenbrenner's bet is that the scarcest input in the AI era is not algorithms — it is **power and the physical compute stack**. If frontier AI keeps scaling, the world needs trillion-dollar clusters, and those clusters need gigawatts of electricity, datacenters, and memory long before they need another model. So rather than buy the AI-model winners (he owns almost no Nvidia, and no Microsoft, Amazon, Google or Meta as core longs), [Situational Awareness LP goes long the "picks and shovels"](https://fortune.com/2026/03/05/leopold-aschenbrenner-ai-hedge-fund-superintelligence-agi-power-companies-crypto-miners/) — power generators, bitcoin miners repurposing their power-and-cooling into AI hosting, memory makers, and neocloud GPU providers — while using large semiconductor put positions to hedge the crowded, richly-valued megacaps. The book is concentrated, options-levered, and has compounded fast. It is also only partially visible: a 13F shows the longs and listed options, not the shorts, the swaps, or any private AI-lab stakes.

## Who is Leopold Aschenbrenner

Aschenbrenner is a German-American prodigy who graduated Columbia at 19, worked on OpenAI's **Superalignment** team, and was [dismissed in April 2024](https://fortune.com/2026/03/05/leopold-aschenbrenner-ai-hedge-fund-superintelligence-agi-power-companies-crypto-miners/) — by his account, for raising AI-security concerns. In June 2024 he published a ~165-page essay, **"Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead"** ([situational-awareness.ai](https://situational-awareness.ai/)), which became required reading in Silicon Valley. Months later he turned it into an investment firm of the same name, seeded by a roster of tech heavyweights — [Patrick and John Collison (Stripe), Daniel Gross and Nat Friedman](https://fortune.com/2026/03/05/leopold-aschenbrenner-ai-hedge-fund-superintelligence-agi-power-companies-crypto-miners/), plus family offices and endowments. He reportedly put nearly his entire net worth into the fund and runs research alongside Carl Shulman.

## The worldview behind the trade

The essay's core claims are the engine of the portfolio:

- **Scaling is predictable and steep.** Aschenbrenner argues that AI capability has improved by "orders of magnitude" (OOMs) of effective compute per year, and extrapolates that trend to **AGI by roughly 2027**, then a fast intelligence explosion toward superintelligence.
- **The buildout is physical.** Getting there means [a "trillion-dollar cluster"](https://situational-awareness.ai/) — datacenters drawing tens of gigawatts. At that scale, the binding constraint stops being chips-on-paper and becomes **electricity, transformers, cooling, land, and power contracts**.
- **It becomes a national project.** He expects compute and AI security to turn into a matter of state — which, if right, means government-scale capital flooding into exactly the power and infrastructure names the fund owns.

You do not have to accept the 2027 timeline to trade the second-order effect: even a *slower* AI buildout still bottlenecks on power and datacenters first. That is the part of the thesis the portfolio is actually expressing.

## The trade: long the bottleneck, hedge the hype

The fund's [Q1 2026 13F](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0002045724&type=13F-HR) lays out a barbell. On the long side, four buckets:

- **Power & energy** — the literal fuel. [Bloom Energy](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/05/05/leopold-aschenbrenners-situational-awareness-fund/) (fuel cells for on-site datacenter power) is the single largest long; alongside it sit Solaris Energy Infrastructure, Babcock & Wilcox, Power Solutions International and T1 Energy.
- **Bitcoin miners → AI hosts** — the cleverest part of the thesis. [Miners already own interconnected, power-dense, cooled sites](https://blockspace.media/insight/situational-awareness-lp-bitcoin-miner-longs/); re-pointing that capacity at AI inference is faster than building greenfield. The book holds IREN, Core Scientific, Riot, CleanSpark, Bitfarms, Bitdeer and HIVE.
- **AI compute / neoclouds** — CoreWeave, Applied Digital, WhiteFiber and SharonAI: GPU capacity sold by the hour.
- **Memory & storage** — Sandisk and Micron (common + calls): the thesis that an inference build-out is as memory-hungry as it is GPU-hungry.

**The most current move — a new neocloud long.** After that 13F, the fund's freshest disclosure is a [Schedule 13G filed May 27, 2026 revealing a 5.6% stake in Nebius Group (NBIS)](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/05/52827719/nebius-stock-jumps-after-situational-awareness-hedge-fund-reveals-stake) — 12.41M Class A shares, crossing the 5% threshold around May 19. Nebius is another AI neocloud, so this doubles down on the same compute bucket as CoreWeave; the reveal sent the stock up roughly 12% and put Situational Awareness among Nebius's largest holders next to BlackRock. Because a 13D/13G is filed within days of crossing 5% rather than 45 days after quarter-end, it is the freshest window into the book between 13Fs — and at the current quote the stake is worth more than any single line in the Q1 13F.

On the short side, the fund held [**$8B+ of notional put exposure across semiconductors**](https://blockspace.media/insight/situational-awareness-lp-bitcoin-miner-longs/) at quarter-end — the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH), Nvidia, Oracle, Broadcom, AMD, plus Micron, TSMC, ASML, Intel and Corning. Read this not as "he hates chips" but as a hedge on the *crowded, expensively-priced* expression of AI, funded by conviction in the *cheap, overlooked* expression.

## How it has performed

By public reporting, the fund's disclosed U.S.-equity book grew from [~$255M at end-2024 to ~$5.5B at end-2025 to ~$13.7B of 13F notional by Q1 2026](https://13f.info/manager/0002045724-situational-awareness-lp) — though note that figure is inflated by option notional. On returns, [Situational Awareness reportedly gained ~47% after fees in the first half of 2025](https://litquidity.co/1-5b-ai-hedge-fund-launches-surges-in-first-year/) versus roughly 6% for the S&P 500, with various outlets citing far larger full-period numbers since. Treat the headline returns with caution: they are **options-amplified, self-reported, and not independently audited**, and a concentrated levered book that triples on the way up can halve on the way down.

## How to shadow it — honestly

This is the part most "copy the 13F" write-ups get wrong:

- **A 13F is a 45-day-old snapshot**, filed well after quarter-end. You are always seeing where the book *was*, not where it *is*.
- **It shows longs and listed options only** — never the short stock, swaps, cash, non-US lines, or any private AI-lab positions. The picture is structurally incomplete.
- **Option notional ≠ exposure.** A "$2B SMH put" line is $2B of *underlying controlled*, not $2B at risk. Several put names (Micron, TSMC, AMD, ASML) sit *opposite* longs/calls on the same ticker — these are spreads, not outright shorts.
- **The replicable part is the directional long book** — the power, miner, compute and memory names held as common stock. That is what the "copy book" on the desk page isolates. The put overlay is context, not a retail trade.
- **Concentration cuts both ways.** The top ten lines are the overwhelming majority of the book. Sizing into the same names without his balance sheet or his hedges means taking far more single-name risk than he is.

## Where the thesis could break

- **Timeline risk.** If AGI-scale scaling stalls or plateaus, the trillion-dollar-cluster demand curve flattens and the power/infra trade unwinds first.
- **A power glut.** Today's bottleneck is tomorrow's overbuild. If utilities, miners and developers all race to add capacity, the scarcity premium in power names compresses.
- **Rate & valuation risk.** Many longs (neoclouds, miners, fuel cells) are long-duration, cash-burning, capital-intensive names that get repriced hard when rates rise or risk appetite fades.
- **The megacaps win anyway.** If Nvidia and the hyperscalers capture most of the AI value, the semiconductor puts bleed while the picks-and-shovels longs lag the very names he is short.
- **Single-manager, single-theme.** This is one concentrated worldview expressed with leverage. It is a high-variance satellite bet, not a core holding — size it accordingly.

## Where it fits in a portfolio

The honest framing: this is a **thematic, high-conviction, high-variance way to express the AI-infrastructure trade** — and a chance to learn from a manager who clearly thinks from first principles. Shadowing the directional longs is reasonable as a small, deliberately-sized satellite sleeve. Mechanically mirroring the whole 13F — puts included — is not, because the disclosure is incomplete and the options lines misrepresent real risk. Use the book as a *map of where smart AI-buildout capital is going*, then size every name to your own risk, not his.
