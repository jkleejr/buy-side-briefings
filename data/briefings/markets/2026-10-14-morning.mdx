## ASML Says AI Chip Demand Will Outrun Its Supply Into 2028, but Memory Stocks Are Still Falling Before CPI

ASML answered half of the question behind five days of chip selling. [It reported record third-quarter sales of €11.7 billion, raised its 2026 sales outlook to €44-45 billion and said its planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet customer demand](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/14/3380319/0/en/asml-reports-11-7-billion-total-net-sales-and-5-0-billion-net-income-in-q3-2026.html). That speaks directly to Hock Tan's warning about who pays for AI: the customers buying lithography tools are still ordering more than ASML can build. It does not speak to the other worry. [Micron traded at $941.31 before the open, extending Tuesday's 3.62% loss, and SK hynix fell another 3.06%](https://www.tipranks.com/news/why-micron-sandisk-and-sk-hynix-stocks-are-falling-in-pre-market-despite-asmls-strong-q3-results), because China's CXMT is a threat to memory prices, not to equipment orders. The chip selling is now splitting by business rather than easing as a whole. An S&P 500 close under 7,666.45 would mean it had reached the broad index anyway.

## What changed since last report

Last night's report named ASML and TSMC as the tests of whether the chip selling was about memory pricing or AI spending; ASML has now answered the spending half, and memory is still being sold.

- **Equipment and memory parted ways.** ASML's capacity shortfall into 2028 is evidence that leading-edge customers are still spending, but it says nothing about DRAM pricing, which is where CXMT competes. That is why ASML rose in Europe while Micron and SK hynix fell again before the US open.
- **Oil is rising on attacks, not lost supply.** [Brent rose to about $106.32 and briefly above $107 after a container ship and a tanker were hit in the Strait of Hormuz](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/14/oil-prices-rise-after-reported-attacks-on-ships-in-strait-of-hormuz), and [a Houthi missile struck an Aramco building in Dhahran](https://sundayguardianlive.com/world/crude-oil-prices-today-october-14-why-are-oil-prices-rising-today-brent-crude-hits-10658-as-saudi-aramco-attack-and-strait-of-hormuz-risks-offset-hopes-of-higher-russian-diesel-exports-305338/). That puts Brent back near Monday's $106.65, about $2 above the $104.7 truce line.
- **Bank results split by business.** Bank of America's miss came from a fine and weak bond trading, [with fixed-income trading revenue down 17%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/bank-of-america-bac-q3-2026-earnings.html), while [Morgan Stanley earned $3.03 a share on $20.3 billion of revenue, up from $18.2 billion a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000895421/000089542126000288/a3q26msearningsrelease.htm). Tuesday's credit-cost worry does not apply to a firm that lives on trading and wealth fees.

## What to watch

**CPI against the ten-year.** The print lands at 8:30 with the ten-year at 5.195%, about 14 basis points under its 5.331% high. A second straight 0.3% core reading would likely put that high back in play and challenge the one-more-hike ceiling New York Fed President Williams described on Tuesday. A 0.2% core reading or lower, with headline inflated by energy, would support the view that the Fed stops after December. [FedWatch put the odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting near 21% going in](https://www.investing.com/central-banks/fed-rate-monitor).

**Memory against TSMC.** Micron near $941 and SK hynix still falling show the CXMT worry has not been answered by ASML. TSMC reports Thursday; unchanged AI revenue guidance would leave the selling confined to memory pricing, while a cut would revive the spending worry that ASML has just eased. The S&P 500 holding above 7,666.45 remains the test of whether the selling stays inside the sector.

**Brent against the truce line.** Brent is about $2 above $104.7, with ship attacks in Hormuz and the Dhahran strike weighed against more Russian diesel. The IEA board meets today and Thursday on the diesel-first reserve release. A settle above Monday's $106.65 would put more energy into the October inflation print; a confirmed Russian energy truce would test $104.7 from above.
