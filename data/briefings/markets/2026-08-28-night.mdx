## September Is Live

The rate path entering Labor Day weekend is the most contested since the July 29 FOMC. The 59% September hike probability is the highest of the cycle, and Warsh has now explicitly said the summer's better inflation data hasn't changed his underlying view. Three July dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) already voted for an immediate hike; Warsh's Jackson Hole signal closes the gap between the chair and the dissenting bloc.

The bond market's curve-flattening response carries the clearest read on where the cycle heads from here. The front end priced the September hike; the long end fell, pricing the eventual slowdown that a hike brings. At 5.17%, the 30Y is 13bps inside the regime trigger — paradoxically, Warsh's hawkish signal pushed it further from 5.30%, not closer. The Bessent buyback program (starting September 9, $4B+ per operation, 10-30yr bonds) provides structural long-end demand that is suppressing the 30Y even in a hawkish environment. Without that bid, the equilibrium 30Y rate on Friday's data would likely sit 10-15bps higher.
