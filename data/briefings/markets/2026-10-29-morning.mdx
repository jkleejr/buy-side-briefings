## Alphabet Heads for Its Worst Post-Earnings Drop and Pulls Nasdaq Futures Down Before PCE and Amazon

Alphabet is down about 10% before the open, and a close more than 9.77% lower would be its worst reaction to an earnings report on record. The selling has spread: Nasdaq-100 futures are down 1.0%–1.3% and chip makers are giving back Wednesday's gains, while Microsoft is up about 3.5% and Dow futures are higher. What happened is a sale of the company carrying the cost of AI building, not of AI demand itself. Google Cloud grew 103%, but free cash flow came in at negative $9.07 billion against roughly negative $2.70 billion expected, and search advertising missed. The evidence cuts both ways for suppliers: Alphabet held its $195–$205 billion capex plan and guided higher for 2027, which is chip revenue, yet chip stocks are falling with it. The read no longer holds if the Nasdaq closes back above 26,424.82 today, or if Amazon's results tonight are rewarded for higher spending.

- [Alphabet fell about 10% premarket, on course for its biggest post-earnings drop if it closes more than 9.77% lower](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/alphabet-google-microsoft-msft-shares-today-earnings.html) — the move would erase roughly $395–$400 billion of market value on press estimates, larger than most S&P 500 companies.
- [Free cash flow of negative $9.07 billion was more than three times the negative $2.70 billion analysts expected, and search advertising grew 12% to $63.35 billion, short of forecasts](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/823825) — the core business that funds the spending missed in the same quarter the spending deepened the cash burn.
- [Nasdaq-100 futures fell about 1.3% and S&P 500 futures 0.5% while Dow futures rose 0.3%, with Microsoft up 3.5% on 48% Azure growth](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/asian-shares-fall-as-alphabet-earnings-miss-drags-wall-st-futures-lower-126102900097_1.html) — the selling is concentrated in AI spenders and suppliers, not spread across the market.
- [AMD fell 3.3%, Intel 3.6% and Nvidia about 1% in early premarket trading](https://invezz.com/news/2026/10/29/why-are-nvidia-amd-and-intel-stocks-crashing-in-pre-market-trading-today/) — chip makers are trading with Alphabet's share price rather than with the capex plan that pays them.
- [September PCE is expected at 3.6% headline and 3.1% core, with third-quarter GDP near 3.2%, both at 8:30am](https://www.investing.com/analysis/treasury-yields-face-2-tests-as-fed-decides-before-gdp-and-pce-200690359) — with the 10-year near 5% and December hike odds at 85.7%, a hot print would add rate pressure to a tech sector already selling off.

## What changed since last report

Last night's report said the market split Microsoft and Alphabet on cost, and the split widened overnight into a sale of chip makers as well.

- **The cash miss was larger than the after-hours move first suggested.** Free cash flow came in more than $6 billion below what analysts expected, and [capex of $51.36 billion was actually under the $55.09 billion estimate](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-alphabet-stock-plunging-today-93CH-4981830). The market is reacting to revenue that did not keep up with spending, not to the spending itself.
- **Asia did not follow chips down evenly.** [The Kospi fell 1.24% as Samsung dropped 4.1% despite a record quarterly profit, while SK Hynix rose 2.6% after a new shareholder-return plan](https://www.ajupress.com/view/20261029153336069). Memory demand is not in question there; how much of it reaches each company's shareholders is.
- **Oil eased on diplomacy.** [Brent fell 0.86% to $114.30 after reports that Vice President Vance contacted Iranian officials](https://arbiterz.com/oil-prices-today-brent-falls-to-114-30-per-barrel-as-us-iran-peace-hopes-ease-supply-fears), taking back part of Wednesday's gain. That removes some pressure from long yields before PCE.

## What to watch

**The Nasdaq against 26,424.82 through Amazon tonight.** A 1% decline at the open would put the Composite near 26,285, under the level that defined this week's breakout, with Monday's 26,169.38 below. Amazon reports after the close with [revenue of about $202.2 billion and EPS of $1.96 expected, and its 2026 capex guidance at $220 billion](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/previews/26/10/62663775/amazon-q3-earnings-preview-all-eyes-aws-after-posting-best-growth-years-q2). AWS growth at or above Q2's 37% with steady cash flow would break the read that spending is being sold; a close under 26,169.38 would erase the week's gain.

**The 10-year between 5% and 5.11% on PCE at 8:30am.** Headline at 3.6% and core at 3.1% are expected. A print above either, with December hike odds at 85.7%, would test 5.11%, the level at which rates take over from earnings as the driver of tech. A softer print would likely take the 10-year back under 5% and give software some relief that chips, now trading on Alphabet, may not share. The ECB decides at 9:15am ET and [is expected to hold](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/ecb-expected-to-hold-202610291016).

**Bitcoin just under its October 8 low.** BTC is at $80,072, below $80,320, after an overnight low of $79,623. Its 1.3% decline matches the drop in Nasdaq futures, so it is trading as a risk asset rather than as a hedge. A recovery above $80,320 by Friday's large monthly options expiry would make this a test that held; a daily close under $79,623 would put the low in question.
