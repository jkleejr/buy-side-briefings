## Why it happened

- **The conflict moved upstream.** Monday's constraint was conversion capacity; Tuesday's target list was distribution and export infrastructure on both sides — Aramco terminals at Abha and Najran that feed Saudi domestic supply, and [Kharg Island, through which the large majority of Iran's crude leaves the country](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/explosions-heard-on-irans-kharg-island-local-media-3000974). A refinery outage raises a margin; an export terminal outage removes barrels.
- **The bid in rates is policy, not energy.** Crude added 2.43% and the curve moved less than two basis points at any point, while the front end moved most. With hike odds near 60% into next week's meeting, the marginal seller of gold and duration is pricing a Fed response to inflation, not the inflation itself.
- **The equity split was AI capex against everything else.** [Oracle rose about 5% intraday and CoreWeave 3% on a Morgan Stanley target increase](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/08/oracle-rallies-5-as-morgan-stanley-lifts-its-price-target-coreweave-advances-3/), which is what held the Nasdaq's loss to a third of the Dow's. The Dow carries no comparable offset, and its 628-point decline against the Russell 2000's 0.52% says the damage was concentrated in large industrial and consumer names rather than in domestic small caps, which are the more rate-sensitive group.
- **This morning's read was right on structure and one step behind on the target.** It called a cost rotation rather than a retreat, and the close matched — Dow down 1.18% against the Nasdaq's 0.32%, with the two-year barely moving. It located the shortage in refining margin; the day's news put it in production and export volumes instead.
- **One threshold broke and three held:** the S&P 500 closed below 7,700. WTI is 1.4% from $95, the ten-year 2.4 basis points from 4.818%, the thirty-year 4.9 from 5.30%.
