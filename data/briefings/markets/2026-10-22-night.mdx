## Nasdaq Has Its Worst Day Since June on Chip and Meta Selling as the 10-Year Falls Below 5%

The Nasdaq fell 2.60% to 26,008.86, its worst day since June, and closed below Tuesday's 26,424.82, so Wednesday's rebound did not hold. The selling was narrow: the Dow ended up 0.02%, the Russell 2000 rose 0.11% and most US stocks gained. Chips and Meta carried the loss. Lam Research said supply shortages would push some shipments out of the December quarter, and the chip index fell more than 6%. Meta lifted its 2026 expense range to $175-179 billion and fell nearly 7%. Bond buyers took the other side: the 10-year fell about 17 basis points to 4.96% even as Brent rose 4%. The S&P 500 closed 3 points below 7,666.45; a close back above 26,424.82 on the Nasdaq would mean today's break did not stick.

## Why it happened

This morning's report said a Nasdaq close below 26,424.82 and an S&P 500 close below 7,666.45 would show Wednesday's rebound failed. Both happened, the Nasdaq by 416 points and the S&P 500 by 3.

- **A supply limit, not a demand miss.** [Lam's December guide of $8.6 billion was 0.35% above consensus, and the CFO said it would have been higher without supply constraints](https://invezz.com/au/news/2026/10/22/lam-research-stock-falls-9percent-despite-record-results-this-analyst-sees-stock-doubling/). Shipments moving into March put a ceiling on next quarter's chip equipment sales, and Applied Materials and ASML fell with it. China, 35% of Lam's September revenue, is expected to fall meaningfully as a share in December.
- **Cost rising faster than the beat.** Meta's revenue grew 26%, but total costs rose 68% to $51.6 billion. The market priced the cost line, not the revenue beat, and Meta gave no 2027 spending figure to cap it.
- **Gold did not get the haven buying.** [Gold fell as safe-haven demand shifted to Treasuries](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-slides-as-safe-haven-demand-shifts-to-us-treasuries-despite-lower-yields-202610221837), losing about 2.4% to near $4,040 even with lower yields and higher oil, which usually support it.
- **Money moved to bonds and to the rest of the market.** [Jobless claims fell to 194,000 against 200,000 expected](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/10/22/treasurys-bonds-ai-yields.html), so the bond rally did not come from labour data. It came from investors leaving tech, and [traders largely priced out a hike next week](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html), with December still expected.
