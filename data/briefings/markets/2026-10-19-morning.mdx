Futures point to a rebound in the AI shares that drove last week's selling, and the clearest cause on the calendar is oil. [Reuters reported Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.99% and S&P 500 futures up 0.47%, with Dow futures flat](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026:newsml_L6N4650KA:0-wall-st-futures-climb-as-ai-stocks-rebound-oil-yields-fall/), after Brent fell about 1.8% on President Trump's waiver of some Russia sanctions. Cheaper oil eases that worry only if it reaches bond yields, and so far it has barely done so: the 10-year sits near 5.08%, about a basis point lower. The futures gain is a bounce after the Nasdaq's 4.5% weekly fall, not yet a change in the rates picture behind it.

## What changed since last report

The Sunday night report read the futures reopening as a pause in the selling, not a reversal; Monday's larger gains extend the pause but do not yet settle that question.

- **Oil fell for a policy reason, not a Hormuz reason.** The Russia waiver added expected supply. Tanker attacks and Gulf exports at under half their pre-war level are unchanged, so the relief rests on one executive order that can be revisited inside 60 days.
- **Rate-hike pricing had already eased before today.** [CME FedWatch](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html) odds of an October 27-28 hike were reported near 15%, down from about 41% after the hot September CPI on October 14. That decline came before Friday's chip selloff, which is why the weekend report doubted rates drove the week, while Reuters still frames the selling around them. Both can hold: hike odds for October fell while the 10-year stayed above 5%.
- **The 10-year has not followed oil.** Near 5.08%, it is little changed from Friday. A stock rebound without a bond rally rests on positioning after a 4.5% weekly fall in the Nasdaq, not on cheaper money for AI borrowers.
