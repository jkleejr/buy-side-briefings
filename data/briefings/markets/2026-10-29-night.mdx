## Alphabet Has Its Worst Day on Record, and Amazon Falls After Hours on the Same Spending Question

Alphabet fell 12.64%, its largest one-day drop on record, removing about $500 billion of market value. The Nasdaq lost about 1.4% and closed roughly 15 points above Monday's 26,169.38. After the close, Amazon fell about 7% even though AWS grew 39%, its fastest in nearly five years, because it said 2027 spending will be "meaningfully more" than this year's $220 billion. Two companies with accelerating cloud growth were sold on what that growth costs. The selling stayed narrow: the Russell 2000 rose 1.01% and the 10-year fell to about 4.98% on in-line inflation, so this was not a rates scare or a broad retreat from stocks. The read no longer holds if the Nasdaq closes back above 26,424.82.

## Why it happened

This morning's report said the Nasdaq would lose 26,424.82 unless Amazon was rewarded for spending more; the index closed under it and Amazon was not rewarded, so that read held. Its bitcoin test did not: the October 8 low gave way.

- **Cash, not demand, is what is being priced.** Amazon's [free cash flow was a $42.4 billion outflow over the past 12 months](https://www.implicator.ai/amazon-spent-77b-property-equipment-q3-leading-free-cash/), while [Jassy put the AWS backlog at $578 billion, more than double a year ago](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/aws-will-be-a-1t-business-jassy-says-but-first-it-will-spend-big/). The evidence cuts both ways: contracted demand is growing faster than the spending, but the cash arrives years after it leaves.
- **The selling reached the companies that supply AI's power.** [Caterpillar beat with $7.20 of adjusted EPS against $7.10 but held its sales forecast and absorbed about $600 million of tariff costs](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/earnings/26/10/62696996/ais-power-hunger-sends-caterpillar-orders-soaring-tariffs-spoil-the-party); closing reports put its drop between 7% and 9.3%, its worst day since March 2020 on the larger figure.
- **Rates stayed out of it.** [December hike odds on CME FedWatch ranged from about 75% to 83% through the day](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-falls-to-near-4-000-as-us-pce-data-reinforces-december-fed-hike-odds-202610292318), little changed from Wednesday's 85.7%, and the 10-year fell despite the GDP beat.
- **Oil eased the pressure on long yields.** [Brent settled 0.38% lower at $114.88 after trading more than $2 down](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-prices-settles-lower-diplomacy-hopes-offset-some-mideast-supply-concerns) on hopes for US-Iran talks, though a senior Iranian official dismissed Qatar's draft deal. Oil is still about 1.6% under Friday's $116.72 high, so the election-week premium has not gone.
