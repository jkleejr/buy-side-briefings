## Brent Reaches a Six-Month High and the Selling Moves From Tech to the Dow

Thursday's losses sat in AI stocks; Friday's sat everywhere else. The Dow fell 652.82 points, or 1.23%, to 52,215.16, while the Nasdaq slipped only 0.21% and the S&P 500 0.52%, with consumer staples, utilities and healthcare all lower. The trigger was oil and prices, not chips: Brent settled up 3.1% at $116.72, its highest close since April 29, after Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites, and the October flash PMI showed US selling prices rising at their fastest since August 2022. The 10-year rose about 5 basis points to 4.999%, which gave back a third of Thursday's fall but stayed below 5.0%. A 10-year above Wednesday's 5.11% before the Fed decides on Wednesday would show oil, not the AI selling, setting rates again.

## Why it happened

This morning's report set the test as the 10-year against 5.0% and 5.11% with Brent above $113. The result was mixed: Brent cleared $113 by $3.72 and the 10-year moved up with it, as the morning warned, but it stopped a basis point short of 5.0% and well short of 5.11%.

- **Oil moved from an Iran risk to a Saudi risk.** Thursday's 4% gain came on reports of renewed US strikes on Iran; Friday's 3.1% added Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and Aramco's evacuation of its Dhahran headquarters. Brent's 6.2% weekly gain against WTI's 2% puts the premium in Gulf seaborne supply.
- **The PMI gave the oil move an inflation reading.** Output prices at 59.8 and manufacturing at a 53-month high of 56.0 show demand strong enough for firms to pass costs through. That is why yields rose with oil rather than falling as they did Thursday.
- **Defensive sectors did not protect.** Staples, utilities and healthcare fell with the rest, partly because they had run furthest: the [staples fund was up about 11% in October through Friday, on track for its best month since its 1998 launch](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/10/62574069/this-week-on-wall-street-staples-ai-bill-gm-fed-hyperscalers), against a 6.9% fall in the tech fund. Money that left tech earlier in the month had less of a safe place to sit once oil rose.
- **The week still ended with lower yields.** The 10-year was down about 11 basis points over five sessions, [a third straight weekly decline](https://rallies.ai/news/us-yields-edge-up-but-10-year-under-5-as-ai-jitters-remain-e14538830efecb78), so Friday reversed only part of the move out of tech and into bonds. The question for next week is which force sets yields when both oil and AI earnings move at once.
- **Consumer sentiment was not the cause.** The final Michigan reading of 48.0 [came in above the 46.3 preliminary](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/bonds-yields-us-treasurys.html), so the selling was not about the household data.

## What to watch

**The 10-year between 4.95% and 5.11% into Wednesday's Fed decision.** Thursday's close and Wednesday's high bracket it. FedWatch odds of an October hike have fallen to [about 7%](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html), so the decision itself is largely priced; the statement on the PMI's price signal is what is not. [CME FedWatch put a December move near 80%](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/10/62574069/this-week-on-wall-street-staples-ai-bill-gm-fed-hyperscalers), and Bank of America still expects a 25 basis point hike. A move above 5.11% with Brent above $113 would show oil-driven inflation setting rates again. A return below 4.95% would show growth worry back in charge.

**Brent against $116.72.** It is the highest settlement since April 29 and up three weeks in a row. The 30-year at 5.312% shows how much of that is reaching long rates. A fall back below $113 would show the Saudi strikes priced as contained; a further rise with Saudi confirmation of the Houthi-claimed hit at Yanbu would put the Saudi supply risk next to the Iran one, not instead of it.

**The Nasdaq between 25,955.34 and 26,424.82.** Two lower closes, but Friday's was 0.21% while the Dow lost 1.23%, so the AI selling did not deepen. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the Fed and Amazon on Thursday, the first hyperscaler results since Meta's spending rise. A close above 26,424.82 would show Thursday's break did not hold; a close below Friday's level on those results would show Meta's cost problem reads across.
