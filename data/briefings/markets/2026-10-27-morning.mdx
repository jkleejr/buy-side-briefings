## Oil Climbs Back to $116 After a Houthi Attack Claim on Saudi Arabia, as the Fed Meeting Opens

Oil has taken back Monday's diplomacy discount. Brent fell about 1% overnight, then rose above $116 after the Houthis said they hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery. That leaves it within about 0.5% of Friday's $116.72 high, the level last night's report said would put oil back in charge of rates. So far rates have not followed: the 10-year is flat at 4.982%, stock futures are up about 0.1%–0.3%, and Asian chip stocks rallied. The evidence cuts both ways. Saudi Arabia says it intercepted three missiles and has not confirmed any damage, so the attack has added risk without taking supply off the market. A Brent settlement above $116.72 with the 10-year through 5% would mean oil is setting rates again before Wednesday's Fed decision. Brent back under $115 would leave Monday's tech rebound intact.

## What changed since last report

Last night's report set Brent against $116.72 and the 10-year against 5%. Overnight, Brent moved back toward its upper level; the 10-year has not moved.

- **Supply risk came back, and diplomacy did not fade.** Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks in Tehran on Monday and agreed to keep talking. That explains the early dip. The Ras Tanura claim and a fire Bloomberg reported near Aramco facilities reversed it within hours.
- **The dollar, not yields, absorbed the move.** The dollar index is [near 102, a two-week high](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-struggles-near-4-000-as-us-dollar-strengthens-ahead-of-fed-decision-202610271107), and gold has slipped to about $4,004, within 1% of its October 23 low of $3,975.23. So far the oil move has shown up in the dollar and gold rather than in Treasury yields.
- **Europe is taking the oil hit; Asia is not.** The FTSE 100 is down about 0.8% after HSBC's results disappointed. Europe imports most of its energy, while Asia's chip exporters are trading Monday's Anthropic-driven compute rally.
