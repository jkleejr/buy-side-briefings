## Why it happened

This morning's report read the futures gain as a bounce after a 4.5% weekly fall, led by analyst calls on beaten-down names, with the 10-year not following oil. That held for stocks and bonds. The oil half did not: Brent gave back almost all of the waiver's drop by the settle, so cheaper oil did not reach rates because by the close it was not cheaper.

- **Analyst calls led the buying.** [Morgan Stanley named Amazon a top pick and raised its target to $350 from $335, citing AWS growth of 37%](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/19/amazon-jumps-5-as-morgan-stanley-names-it-top-pick-on-faster-aws-growth-microsoft-and-alphabet-edge-higher/); Intel and Datadog rose on upgrades in the morning. The day added recommendations, not new demand or financing data.
- **Bonds gave no help.** The 10-year barely moved and the 30-year rose about 4 basis points. Without lower long rates, the borrowing cost behind the AI building plans is unchanged, which is why CoreWeave did not rally with the chipmakers. The same rates are why builders cut prices: the NAHB tied October's drop to mortgage rates "well above 7%", so a 10-year held above 5% is now weighing on housing as well as AI.
- **Oil held near $110 on a second supply threat.** The Houthis said they hit an Aramco facility at Yanbu and the Shaybah field; Saudi Arabia did not confirm it and Reuters could not verify it. WTI's gain was partly thin trading before the November contract expires.
- **Europe fell on France, not on AI.** [Weekend riots pushed French 10-year yields up and widened the gap to German debt, a day before a budget vote](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/19/france-protests-bond-yields-markets.html). The DAX fell 1.18% while US chips rose, so the overseas selling had a separate cause.

## What to watch

**The S&P 500 against 7,666.45.** Monday's close is 24 points above it. Holding there through Wednesday, with the Dow stable, would make Friday's break look like an options-expiry move. A close back below it, especially after Netflix or Meta disappoint, would show the rebound was positioning. This resolves by Thursday.

**CoreWeave against its 52-week low.** It is the clearest test of whether the market has stopped worrying about AI debt. Chips rising while CoreWeave makes new lows means buyers are picking the strongest balance sheets, not betting on the whole buildout. A close above Friday's level with credit swaps narrowing would be the first sign the financing stress is easing. CoreWeave reports in mid-November.

**The 10-year between 5.0% and 5.331%.** The yield has stayed above 5% through a 4.5% Nasdaq fall and a builder index at a 14-year low, so weaker data is not pulling it down. A move under 5.0% would ease both housing and AI borrowing; a move toward 5.331%, the regime line, would put rates back at the centre before the October 27-28 Fed meeting. Thursday's jobless claims are the main data test.
