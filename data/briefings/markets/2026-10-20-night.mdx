## The Dow and Small Caps Rose While Tech Stood Still, Then SpaceX and Super Micro Reported Strong AI Demand After the Close

The Dow rose 1.02% and the Russell 2000 1.65%, while the Nasdaq slipped 0.02%. Investors again rewarded companies with reported results, such as 3M and the drugmakers, over AI names. Lower oil and a 10-year yield down about 3 basis points helped the shares that depend on rates. After the close came the AI demand figures the Nasdaq had been waiting for. SpaceX's revenue beat estimates by almost $2 billion as its AI business grew more than ninefold, and Super Micro raised its quarterly revenue range by more than a quarter. Both rose in late trading; Netflix fell. These are figures from two companies, not yet a change in the AI financing worry. Meta's capital spending plans on Wednesday are a larger test, and a Nasdaq close below Tuesday's 26,424.82 after them would show the after-hours gains did not last.

## Why it happened

This morning's report expected the AI selling to continue and an S&P 500 test of 7,666.45. That was half right. AI names did not lead, and the Dow rose as the report said, but the S&P 500 closed 46 points above the line, and CoreWeave recovered a 5% premarket fall to [close up about 1.65% at $62.13](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crwv/).

- **Reported profits drew the buying.** [3M rose 6.09% to $171.88 after raising its full-year profit range to $9.10-9.15 a share](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/3m-lifts-annual-profit-forecast-on-cost-controls-price-hikes-4959541), the largest single lift to the Dow. Drugmakers took health care to a record. A company that reported strong results and raised guidance was rewarded; an AI name with only a promise was not.
- **A peace report lowered oil, yields and the dollar together.** A report of a Qatar-Pakistan compromise briefly pushed oil down as much as 3.3% and the dollar to its lowest since October 1. Brent then recovered most of the drop after the denial. The same move took about 3 basis points off the 10-year, which helped small caps, since they borrow at floating rates more than large companies do.
- **Tech started the day behind.** Seoul's 3.73% fall, led by SK hynix and Samsung, and SoftBank's drop of more than 6% in Tokyo put AI hardware under pressure before the US open. The chip index then [bounced near its summer low around 11,000](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-dow-rises-sp-500-nasdaq-climb-as-dip-buying-resumes-165236471.html) and closed 0.47% higher. Buyers defended that level, but the gain was a third of Monday's.
- **The after-hours figures cut both ways.** [SpaceX's AI segment brought in $7.56 billion, up 830%, and earned $431 million](https://investinglive.com/stocks/spacex-beats-q3-forecasts-across-all-three-segments-as-ai-revenue-tops-7-5-billion/), its first quarter as the largest segment. AI capital spending of $16.4 billion came in slightly below the roughly $16.9 billion expected. Total spending still exceeded revenue, so the results show demand for AI computing, not that building it has become cheaper to fund.
- **Fed pricing did not move.** [CME FedWatch put an October 27-28 hike at about 17% and a December hike at about 90%](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/20/another-rate-hike-is-expected-before-the-end-of-2026-why-it-might-have-a-much-bigger-impact-on-the-markets-this-time/), little changed from Monday. Weak housing has not yet changed what the market expects from the Fed.

## What to watch

**Whether SpaceX and Super Micro move the Nasdaq.** Both reported AI demand above estimates after the close. If those gains carry into Wednesday's session and the Nasdaq closes above Monday's 26,430.89 while the Dow holds, the market is again paying for AI spending that shows up as revenue. If the Nasdaq stays flat while the after-hours gains fade, buyers still want reported profits more than AI growth. This resolves on Wednesday, before Meta reports.

**Meta's capital spending plans against its five-day slide.** Options imply about a 6% move after Wednesday's results, [per Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/options/26/10/62480628/meta-platforms-tesla-could-swing-more-than-100-billion-after-earnings). The question is whether higher spending comes with revenue growth that pays for it. Spending that rises faster than revenue would bring back the funding worry CoreWeave carries. CoreWeave closing back below Monday's 52-week low would confirm that. Lam Research's results the same evening test equipment orders.

**The 10-year between 5.0% and 5.331%, and Brent near $109.** The yield eased on a peace report that was then denied, so the drop rests on a headline, not on data. A confirmed deal is the clearest route under 5.0%. A Brent settle back above Tuesday's intraday high near $112 would leave yields where they were before the Fed meeting. Thursday's jobless claims and flash PMIs are the next data tests.
