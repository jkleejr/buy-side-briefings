## What changed since last report

- **Iran's reopening acquired conditions and a delivery date.** The seven-day timetable was already on the calendar from Monday's reporting; what is new is that Tehran attached terms — an easing of military pressure and the lifting of the port blockade — and that the proposal reached Washington through mediators on September 16, a week before the UN meetings. Set against Trump's post-midterm framing, the two sides are working from clocks roughly seven weeks apart, and crude falling a sixth day says the market is discounting the earlier one.
- **The financial track escalated on the same morning.** Treasury's sanctions grounding Iranian airlines worldwide [take effect today](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0623), working through secondary designations on the foreign airports, fuel suppliers and ticketing firms that service them rather than on the carriers. It raises the cost to Tehran of holding out without removing a barrel from the market — which is why the same week can carry both a de-escalation trade in crude and a tightening squeeze on Iran.
- **A second diplomatic track now reaches the equity market's leadership.** Xi is in Washington ahead of Thursday's bilateral. The semiconductor complex has risen six sessions running and Nvidia's current forecast assumes no data-center chip sales to China, so export-licence language from the summit repricing that assumption cuts in either direction.
- **Continuity:** yesterday's report found that a fifth session of falling crude bought no rate relief; a sixth has not either. The levels that define it are unchanged — the ten-year at 4.959% against last week's 5.041% nineteen-year high, and Brent at $98.57 against the $100 level broken Monday.
