## Wholesale Prices Jumped the Most Since 2022, Yet Yields Fell and the Selling Rotated Out of Chips Into the Dow

September producer prices rose 1.3% on the month, the largest gain since March 2022, and the bond market bought Treasuries anyway. [The ten-year fell about 10 basis points to roughly 5.10%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/us-treasury-yields-investors-economic-data-.html) and October hike odds held near 32%, where they were before the release. Stocks followed the same split. The S&P 500 fell as much as 0.64%, to about 7,670, within four points of 7,666.45, then closed up 0.33%. The Dow rose 1.08% and the Russell 2000 1.38%, while the chip index fell another 0.88%. A hot inflation print that moved neither yields nor hike odds says the AI selling is no longer being driven by rates. Money left AI hardware for the rest of the market rather than leaving stocks. A close under 7,666.45 would mean that rotation had turned into broad selling.

- [Final-demand PPI rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% on the year, the largest 12-month gain since December 2022, with energy up 10.8% and accounting for over 80% of the rise](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_10152026.htm) — but the measure excluding food, energy and trade services is at 5.0% on the year, so the pressure is not only oil.
- [Retail sales rose 0.8% against 0.3% expected, but the control group rose only 0.1% against 0.3%, and sales excluding gas stations 0.2%](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/us-retail-sales-beat-expectations-in-september-on-higher-gasoline-prices-4951732) — households are paying more for fuel and spending less on everything else.
- [CME FedWatch odds of an October hike stayed at about 31-34% after the data](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/us-treasury-yields-investors-economic-data-.html), with at least one more quarter-point hike still priced by year-end — the hot print did not bring an October move closer.
- [Nvidia's Jensen Huang said CoreWeave's "cost of capital is actually decreasing" and that it needs no backstop](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-ceo-says-coreweave-needs-no-backstop-as-shares-extend-slide-4952315), after its five-year default insurance hit a record 1,194.5 basis points. CoreWeave reversed a 9.5% morning drop to close up 5.94%.
- [The Dow rose 1.08% to 52,177.69 and the Russell 2000 1.38%, while the Nasdaq gained 0.08% and Nvidia fell 1.84%](https://ts2.tech/en/dow-jumps-1-1-russell-2000-rises-1-4-as-broad-rotation-beats-tech/) — a 1-point gap between the Dow and the Nasdaq in the Dow's favour.

## Why it happened

This morning's report said a fall in tech on a soft PPI would show the selling was not about rates. PPI came in hot instead, and yields still fell, which makes the same point more strongly: the read that this is about AI financing and valuation, not the cost of money, held up. The morning's worry that the S&P 500 would break 7,666.45 did not.

- **Investors sold AI hardware and bought bonds with the proceeds.** [Funds moved into Treasuries as chips fell, taking long-term yields to their lowest since September 24](https://qz.com/sp500-nasdaq-tech-selloff-wholesale-inflation-101526). That demand outweighed a 6.3% PPI, which is why yields fell on a report that would normally push them up.
- **The PPI beat was mostly energy, and the market treated it that way.** Energy's 10.8% jump explains over 80% of the gain, and weak control-group sales argue that fuel costs are squeezing other spending. Both make a hike harder to justify, which is why FedWatch odds did not move.
- **The credit-led selling eased after Huang spoke.** CoreWeave's turn from -9.5% to +5.94% followed his remarks, and the chip index, down as much as 4.4% intraday, closed off 0.88%.
- **Software and banks absorbed the money.** [ServiceNow rose 6.14% and Autodesk 6.07% while Lam Research fell 3.30%](https://avaloncapitalresearch.substack.com/p/closing-bell-dow-climbs-11-as-software). Within tech, investors are moving from the companies that supply AI to the ones that use it.
- **The same split showed up overseas.** Korea's Kospi fell 7.57% and the Nikkei about 4.9% on chipmakers overnight, while [the Stoxx 600 rose 0.38% and the FTSE 100 1.08%](https://www.pantheregroup.com/2026/10/15/major-european-markets-close-on-firm-note-7/). Markets heavy in chip stocks fell; markets heavy in banks, energy and healthcare rose.

**CoreWeave's credit against its stock.** Its default insurance at 1,194.5 basis points implied over a 60% chance of default over five years before Huang spoke. If the shares hold while the spread stays near its record, the rebound rests on Huang's words, not on cheaper borrowing.
