## AI Stocks Led a 1% Nasdaq Gain on Reported Revenue, Then Meta Beat and Kept Its Spending Range

The Nasdaq rose 1.05% to 26,702.86, closing above Monday's 26,430.89, as Super Micro and SpaceX extended Tuesday's after-hours gains. Buyers paid for AI revenue that has been reported, the same kind of evidence that drew them to 3M and the drugmakers earlier in the week. After the close Meta's revenue of $64.6 billion beat estimates by about $1.3 billion, and its 2026 spending range rose only at the bottom, to $135-145 billion. Rates gave no help: the 10-year held near 5.11% and Brent near $109. Meta's free cash flow was still negative, so revenue is answering the funding question for now, not cheaper building. A Nasdaq close back below 26,424.82 would mean the rebound did not hold.

## Why it happened

This morning's report said a Nasdaq close above 26,430.89 would show buyers paying again for AI revenue. That held: the index closed 272 points above it, and the gain came without lower rates, as the report expected.

- **Revenue that has been reported, not forecast.** SpaceX's [AI segment brought in $7.56 billion, up 830%](https://investinglive.com/stocks/spacex-beats-q3-forecasts-across-all-three-segments-as-ai-revenue-tops-7-5-billion/), and Super Micro lifted its range by 27% at the midpoint. Those are orders and sales, the evidence that had drawn buyers to companies with reported profits all week.
- **The chip selling had already run far.** The chip index fell eight straight sessions through October 16 and was still about 14% below its October 6 close on Tuesday. A strong result met shares that had already been marked down, which is why Seoul's memory makers rose 5% in one session.
- **Rates were neutral, not supportive.** The 10-year eased about a basis point and [the market priced about a 15% chance of an October 28 hike](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html), with December still expected. Brent's $2 jump on Iran's comments and an [EIA crude draw of about 3.1 million barrels](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/), well above the roughly 820,000 expected, kept oil from helping.
- **Meta answered the narrow question.** Capital spending rose to $34.9 billion from $19.4 billion a year earlier, but revenue grew 26% and the fourth-quarter range sits around estimates. A $9.9 billion legal charge cut reported earnings to $3.83 a share, and free cash flow was about -$2.1 billion.

Lam's after-hours drop is a test of chip equipment, the part of the trade that had risen furthest, with the stock up nearly 80% in a year. A Nasdaq close back below 26,424.82 would show the rebound rested on two companies' results. A second close above 26,700 would show the buying is spreading. Thursday's session settles it.

**Meta's spending after the January outlook.** Meta has not given a 2027 spending number and usually gives one with its fourth-quarter results in late January. This quarter's revenue growth covered higher spending, but free cash flow was negative, which leaves Meta funding part of its buildout from cash or borrowing. Meta's after-hours gain fading below Wednesday's close at Thursday's open would show the market weighting the cash flow over the revenue.

**The 10-year between 5.0% and 5.331%, with oil the swing factor.** The yield eased only about a basis point on a day stocks rose 1%, and Brent near $109 with a larger-than-expected crude draw argues against a fall. A Brent settle above Tuesday's intraday high of $111.99 would raise the odds of a test of 5.331%. A confirmed US-Iran deal remains the clearest route below 5.0%.
