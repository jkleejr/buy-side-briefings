## Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile Had Their Worst Days in 13 to 26 Years After SpaceX Bought Nationwide Spectrum, While the S&P 500 Rose

The three national carriers lost 8.75% to 13.27% on a day the S&P 500 gained 0.59% and closed 7 points under its record. The cause was one transaction: SpaceX agreed to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum, read as Starlink Mobile competing directly for phone subscribers. The evidence cuts both ways. Spectrum is not a network, the deal needs FCC approval, and JPMorgan analysts see limited near-term risk. Against that, losses this size suggest the market is pricing slower long-run growth and less pricing power, not one weak quarter. If the carriers recover half the losses before the FCC sets a review timeline, the move priced the headline; further losses would mean the threat is being priced as permanent.

- [Verizon fell 8.75%, its worst day since July 2002; AT&T fell 9.81%, its worst since 2000; T-Mobile fell 13.27%, its worst since 2013](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/verizon-att-tmobile-stocks-spacex-network.html). The losses deepened from 5-7% premarket to 9-13% at the close, so the selling grew through the day rather than fading.
- [The portfolio includes up to 14 megahertz of paired 800 MHz spectrum, reported at about $8 billion, and FCC Chair Brendan Carr said the agency will oversee the approval](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/09/spacex-starlink-mobile-carrier/). Low-band spectrum reaches indoors and over long distances, the coverage a satellite-only service cannot provide.
- [Verizon said spectrum alone "does not provide the network or infrastructure" to compete with mobile providers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/verizon-att-tmobile-stocks-spacex-network.html). That is the main argument against the selloff: building a nationwide network takes years and capital the carriers already spent.
- [The S&P 500 rose 0.59% to 7,811.54, the Dow 0.83% to 51,654.95 and the Nasdaq 0.64% to 27,366.17](https://rollingout.com/2026/10/09/stock-market-climbs-as-t-mobile-att/). The carriers' losses were confined to one industry; the broad market ended the week higher.
- [Consumer sentiment fell to 46.3 in October from 48.1, the lowest since May's record low of 44.8](http://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/). Gasoline near $4.37 a gallon is the main drag on households, and stocks rose anyway.

## Why it happened

This morning's report was right on the carriers, whose losses roughly doubled from premarket, and on chips recovering part of Thursday's loss; it did not anticipate the S&P 500 closing 0.09% under its record.

- **The carriers fell on accumulation, not one deal.** SpaceX already holds 65 megahertz of AWS-3, AWS-4 and H-block spectrum from [its roughly $17 billion EchoStar purchases](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/34678028/spacex-to-buy-echostar-spectrum-for-17b-powering-starlink-direct-to-cell-expansion). Nationwide low-band adds coverage to that capacity, the two ingredients of a mobile network, so the smaller deal read as the step that made the threat credible. Tower owners, potential landlords to SpaceX, rose 6-8% premarket.
- **OpenAI's year-end figure reversed Thursday's AI selloff.** [Bloomberg reported that OpenAI told investors it expects annualized revenue of $70 billion or more by the end of 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-micron-stocks-bounce-openai-133435783.html), against the roughly $50 billion run rate the FT reported on Thursday. The gap now reads as timing and net-versus-gross accounting, and Nvidia and Micron rose from the open.
- **Oil eased on a political pledge, not on supply.** [Brent fell about 0.7% to near $103.5](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/10/09/oil-falls-trump-comments-iran-talks-ease-supply-concerns/92167970007/) after President Trump ruled out striking Iran before the November 3 midterms; with the Hormuz blockade unchanged, Brent held above $100.
- **Rates did not move.** [The ten-year ended near 5.24%, up about 1 basis point](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/us-treasurys-bond-yields-midterms.html), so Friday's gains came without help from bonds.
