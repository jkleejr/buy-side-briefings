## The Nasdaq Had Its Worst Day Since Mid-August on a Report That OpenAI's Revenue Is Lower Than Thought, While Yields Fell

A Financial Times report that OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue is near $50 billion, about $20 billion below the figure that had circulated, sent the chip index down 3.4% and the Nasdaq down 1.25%. Wednesday's selling followed yields. Thursday's did not: the ten-year eased about 5 basis points to roughly 5.23% after a solid thirty-year auction, and tech fell anyway. That points to doubt about AI revenue, not borrowing costs. One report does not show AI hardware demand slowing: OpenAI's figure is net revenue, and the $70 billion figure apparently came from outsiders using a gross measure. If chip stocks recover their losses before CPI on October 14, the selling was about one headline, not a change in how AI demand is valued.

- [The Nasdaq fell 1.25% to 27,193.34, its worst day since mid-August, and the S&P 500 0.47% to 7,765.36, while the Dow rose 0.1% to 51,231.64](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/investing/openai-nasdaq-stocks). The declines were concentrated in the AI supply chain, not the broad market.
- [The FT reported OpenAI's annualized revenue near $50 billion against about $70 billion previously reported, a gap a source attributed to comparisons with Anthropic using gross cloud-provider revenue](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/08/investing/openai-nasdaq-stocks). The difference is partly about how revenue is measured, not only about weaker sales.
- [Nvidia fell 2.9%, Micron 4.8%, Intel 5.3% and Oracle 5.5%, with Broadcom and Oracle also lower on reports of large financing plans tied to OpenAI](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/345024/SP-500-Nasdaq-end-lower-as-crude-prices-jump-chip-stocks-weigh). The worst losses came in companies whose revenue depends most on OpenAI's spending.
- [The $22 billion thirty-year sold at 5.618% with a 2.54 bid-to-cover and 72.3% to indirect bidders](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/announcements-data-results/), against a 2.41 six-auction average. The week's second long-dated sale found buyers at these yields.
- [The ten-year fell about 5 basis points to near 5.23% and the thirty-year about 6 to 5.602% after the sale](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/currencies/articles/us-bonds-fall-lifting-yields-162459356.html), off Wednesday's 2002-era highs, so lower rates did not support tech.

## Why it happened

This morning's report was right on oil, which held near $104, and wrong on yields, which fell instead of pressing Wednesday's highs. Stocks fell as it expected, but for a reason it did not have: the OpenAI report.

- **The AI trade carried its own question.** A $20 billion gap in the largest AI customer's revenue raises the question of how its spending commitments with Nvidia, Broadcom and Oracle get paid for. The losses followed exposure to OpenAI: Oracle and Micron fell 5-5.5%, the broad S&P 500 under 0.5%.
- **Rates were not the driver.** The ten-year fell from a 5.331% intraday high, and the Dow, with less tech, ended higher. On Wednesday the Russell 2000's 1.31% drop moved with yields. On Thursday tech fell while yields fell.
- **Oil held most of its gain after a de-escalation pledge.** [Oil jumped more than 5%, then pared gains after President Trump said the US will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterms](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/oil-rises-middle-east-supply-012532668.html). Hormuz tanker attacks and hurricane shut-ins kept it near its high.
- **The labour market is not giving the Fed a reason to wait.** [Initial claims fell to 197,000](https://www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf), against about 200,000 expected, and [FedWatch puts December hike odds near 69%](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html).

## What to watch

**Chip stocks and the OpenAI number.** The chip index fell 3.4% on one report. If Nvidia and Micron recover most of Thursday's losses by Tuesday, the market has treated the gap as a measurement question. A second down day, or a Broadcom or Oracle financing disclosure that ties more debt to OpenAI's commitments, would show the market is reconsidering what OpenAI can pay for. Nvidia's 2.9% loss against Micron's 4.8% and Oracle's 5.5% is the measure to track: if the gap widens, the market is pricing risk by how much each company depends on one customer. Resolves over the next three sessions.

**The S&P 500 against 7,666.** The index is 0.7% below its October 6 record of 7,818.93 at 7,765.36, about 99 points above 7,666.45. A close under that level would show weakness spreading from AI suppliers to the broad index. Holding it through CPI with tech recovering would leave Thursday as a sector sell-off, not a change in the market's direction.

**Oil and the 10-year into CPI.** Brent near $104 with the ten-year at 5.23% is an unusual mix: higher oil, lower yields. A hot September core reading on October 14 would bring the ten-year back toward 5.331% and test the 69% December hike odds. Governor Waller said this week that [further hikes need not come at consecutive meetings](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/feds-waller-more-hikes-needed-but-there-is-flexibility-about-the-pace-4938165), so a soft print would more likely push a hike past October 28 than remove it. Brent back under $100, from the IEA's October 14-15 release details or a quieter Gulf, would take pressure off.
