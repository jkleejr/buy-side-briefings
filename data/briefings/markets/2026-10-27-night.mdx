## Chip Stocks Rise a Third Day as Suppliers Help Fund Their AI Customers, and the Nasdaq Clears 26,424

The Nasdaq closed at 26,477.81, up 1.18%, above the 26,424.82 level the last two reports said would show October's tech selling ending, a day before the big-tech results meant to test it. The semiconductor index rose 3.60%, its third straight gain. Today's buying followed financing more than demand: Broadcom arranged $63 billion of debt so Anthropic can lease its chips, and Nvidia and AMD put $4 billion into Meta's share sale. That eases the funding worry Elon Musk raised on Monday, but the chipmakers now stand behind their customers' purchases. Participation was narrow, with 239 new Nasdaq lows against 62 new highs and volume below average. A close back under 26,424.82 after Wednesday's results would put the index back in its range.

- [Broadcom, Bank of America, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone lined up $63 billion for a vehicle that buys Broadcom chips and leases them to Anthropic](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/27/wall-street-banks-blackstone-line-up-63-billion-to-finance-broadcom-chips-for-anthropic) — Broadcom backstops the senior debt, so part of the risk of this sale stays with the seller.
- [Nvidia committed $3 billion and AMD $1 billion to Meta's equity offering, priced at $625 a share](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/nvidia-invest-meta.html) — two suppliers are helping fund a raise of about $97.5 billion that pays for their own hardware.
- [The semiconductor index rose 3.60% to 11,482.51 and the S&P 500 0.60% to 7,722.60, but the Nasdaq had 239 new lows against 62 new highs on below-average volume](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-tuesday-october-27-2026) — the gain came from a small group of large stocks.
- [Brent settled down 1.7% at $113.88 and WTI fell 1.8% to $99.90 on renewed Middle East diplomacy](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/oil-today-iran-us-crude-wti-brent-trump.html) — oil moved away from Friday's $116.72, which gave chip stocks room to rise.
- [Fed funds futures price about a 10% chance of a hike on Wednesday and better than 80% for December](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html) — the rate backdrop that drove October's tech selling has not eased.

## Why it happened

This morning's report turned on Brent against $116.72 and said a move back under $115 would leave Monday's tech rebound intact. That read held: Brent settled at $113.88, the 10-year stayed near 4.99%, and chips extended their gains.

- **Suppliers financed the buyers.** Broadcom is giving the lenders [residual value support](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/broadcom-lines-63-billion-finance-125752921.html) on the senior debt, with deliveries from 2027. This is its second such deal this year, after a $35 billion one. Musk's point on Monday was that lenders were backing away from AI buyers. Today the chipmakers took on part of that risk themselves, so the funding question has moved from the buyers to the sellers.
- **Oil gave the room.** WTI closed under $100, and energy was the weakest sector, down over 2%. Lower oil kept the 10-year flat even after strong data.
- **Strong data, no bond reaction.** [Consumer confidence rose 7.5 points to 91.7, its highest since June](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/us-consumer-confidence-increased-in-october-302920731.html), which the Conference Board tied to cheaper gasoline. [Durable goods orders rose 1.8% against 0.6% expected](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/adv/pdf/durgd.pdf). Both support a December hike, and bonds did not reprice it.

## What to watch

**The Nasdaq against 26,424.82 through Amazon on Thursday.** The level was cleared before the results that were meant to test it, and with more new lows than highs. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the Fed. Higher spending plans would show the large buyers paying for more compute. A close back under 26,424.82 would put the index back in its range, and under Monday's 26,169.38 it would erase this week's gain.

**The 10-year against 5% and 5.11% at Wednesday's 2pm decision.** A hold is about 90% priced. The open question is the more than 80% December hike, and today's confidence and durable goods data give Chair Warsh no reason to rule it out. A 10-year above 5.11% would put rates back in charge of tech. Pushback on December would likely take it below 4.95%.

**Meta against its $625 offering price.** It closed at $620.19, below the price at which Nvidia and other buyers bought in. [Banks plan up to about $21 billion of bonds from the Broadcom deal starting next year](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/banks-eye-21-billion-bonds-200929570.html). Moody's has warned that the risk rises if several such deals are done in a short period, and AMD's arrangement with Anthropic, [more than $20 billion of expected compute purchases alongside up to $5 billion of AMD equity commitments](https://protos.com/inside-amd-and-anthropics-chips-for-shares-deal/), follows the same pattern. If Meta stays under $625 and these bonds price wide, the market is charging more for supplier-backed funding.
