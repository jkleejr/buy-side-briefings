## What changed since last report

Last night's report said a Brent move above $102 on further attacks would put the inflation input back behind the long end. That happened within hours, and the auction-driven decline in yields has been mostly reversed.

- **Oil came from geopolitics and weather together.** The Axios report on Iran strike readiness, the Qatar tanker attack and the Gulf hurricane shut-ins all hit supply at once. The IEA's diesel-first release, which capped Brent at $100.20 on Wednesday, has not been sized yet.
- **The Fed did not add to it.** Waller's remarks restated what the minutes said on Wednesday, and October hike odds near 18% did not move. The ten-year's rise of about 5 basis points came without a change in the expected path of the short rate, which leaves inflation compensation and the price of holding long bonds as the explanation.
- **Equities are responding more than on Wednesday.** Futures are down 0.5-0.9% against Wednesday morning's 0.14-0.58%, with the [Stoxx 600 about 1% lower](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html). Two days of yields near 2002 highs is starting to reach the index, not only small caps.
