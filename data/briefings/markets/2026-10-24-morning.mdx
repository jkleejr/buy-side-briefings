## What the week settled, and what it did not

- **The selling moved, it did not end.** Thursday's losses sat in AI stocks; Friday's sat in the Dow, with staples, utilities and healthcare lower as well. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted a second weekly loss.
- **Oil and AI pull yields in opposite directions.** Money leaving tech pushed the 10-year down about 11 basis points over the week. Oil and Friday's flash PMI, which showed prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022, pushed it back up on Friday. Neither force has won.
- **Bitcoin is following tech, not oil.** It was near $80,813 Saturday, down 1.2% in 24 hours, with a low of $80,445. That is about $125 above the $80,320 level recent reports watched. Crypto.com's daily candles show an intraday low of $79,780 on October 15, so the level has already been broken once, briefly, without a follow-through.
- **What is uncertain.** Houthi claims about damage to Saudi facilities have not been confirmed by Saudi officials. This run could not open the articles behind its sources, and the dollar index was unavailable.

## What to watch

**Brent's Sunday evening open against $113 and $116.72.** An open well below $113 would show the market treating the Saudi attacks as contained. A confirmed hit on Saudi export facilities would add to the premium rather than replace the Iran risk.

**The 10-year between 4.95% and 5.11% into Wednesday.** [PCE inflation for September is due Wednesday morning, with the Fed decision that afternoon](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/article-wall-streets-week-ahead-earnings-flood-fed-meeting-pose-pivotal-tests/). A move above 5.11% with Brent above $113 would show oil-driven inflation setting rates. A return below 4.95% would show growth worry back in charge.

**The Nasdaq between 25,955.34 and 26,424.82.** [Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the close and Amazon on Thursday, part of a week in which about a third of S&P 500 companies report](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/article-wall-streets-week-ahead-earnings-flood-fed-meeting-pose-pivotal-tests/). Spending plans that hold up would argue against the slowdown chip prices have priced in. Cuts would support it.
