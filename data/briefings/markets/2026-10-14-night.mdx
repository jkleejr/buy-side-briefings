## Hotter Core Inflation Raised the Odds of an October Hike, and the AI Companies That Borrow Most Fell Hardest

September's core inflation came in above forecast for a second month, and the market sold the companies that need cheap borrowing to build AI. [Core CPI rose 0.3%, as it did in August](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), and [the odds of a hike at the October 28 meeting roughly doubled to about 44%](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/10/14/us-treasury-yields-cpi-inflation-report.html). The ten-year did not hold its rise: it gave back the jump and ended near Tuesday's level. The selling therefore came from short-term rate expectations and from credit markets, not from long-term yields. [CoreWeave fell about 16%, Dell 11% and Oracle 9%](https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2026/10/14/ai-stocks-drop-after-hot-inflation-report-fuels-bets-for-rate-hikes/), while the Dow ended nearly flat. The S&P 500's 1.1% fall leaves it 0.7% above 7,666.45. A close under that level would mean the selling had spread beyond AI and chips.

- [Headline CPI rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% on the year, against 3.6% expected; core rose 2.6% on the year, against 2.5%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-report-september-2026.html). Gasoline explains the headline beat but not the core beat — the part the Fed sets policy on came in above forecast for a second month.
- [The ten-year jumped 10 basis points to 5.323% after the release, 0.8 basis points short of its 5.331% high](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/10/14/us-treasury-yields-cpi-inflation-report.html), then [slid back to about 5.19% as stocks fell, while the two-year rose to 4.808%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/us-treasury-yields-cpi-inflation-report.html). Short-term yields rose while long-term yields fell back, so investors are betting on a hike now and slower growth later.
- [CoreWeave's bonds traded at yields near 15%, and Oracle's 2032 bond yielded 7.24%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/coreweave-stock-bond-yields-ai-debt.html) — lenders are charging the most-indebted AI builders more, a day after the hike odds doubled.
- [The PHLX semiconductor index fell 4.3%, extending a six-session slide to 11.6%](https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/10/15/us-stocks-fall-on-cpi-shock-as-chip-index-tumbles-43-percent) — the largest one-day loss of the run came on a day when ASML's demand outlook had lifted European chip stocks.
- [The S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq 1.88%, while the Dow lost only 0.09%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/stock-market-today-live-updates-.html) — the index's worst day since July.

## Why it happened

This morning's report was right that a 0.3% core reading would take the ten-year back toward 5.331%, and wrong that ASML had contained the chip selling to memory makers; the chip index fell 4.3% anyway.

- **Higher odds of an October hike raised AI borrowing costs.** [Higher borrowing costs make data centers more expensive to finance, and Oracle's dollar bonds also fell](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/ai-infrastructure-firms-slump-as-us-rate-hike-bets-rise-4949759). The companies that fund AI spending with debt fell the most, and chipmakers fell with them because they sell to those companies.
- **The core beat was not only gasoline.** [Gasoline rose 9.1% and was more than half of the headline gain](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm), but core matched August's 0.3%. That beat is what moved hike odds, and why the two-year held its rise while the ten-year did not. [Futures now price a better than 95% chance of at least one more hike this year](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/10/14/us-treasury-yields-cpi-inflation-report.html), so the open question is timing — October or December — not direction.
- **Dell was downgraded on the same morning.** [Barclays cut Dell to equal weight, citing valuation and little enterprise AI growth so far](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40444247/wall-st-tumbles-as-hot-cpi-boosts-rate-hike-bets). That added a second reason to sell AI server makers on top of financing costs.
- **Outside AI, money moved into safer stocks rather than out of the market.** The Dow's 0.09% loss and [a 0.43% gain in the Stoxx 600 led by ASML](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/european-shares-edge-up-after-solid-asml-results-healthcare-gains-ce785dd2d188f623) show the selling was concentrated, not general.

**The S&P 500 against 7,666.45.** The index is 52 points above it. [Thursday brings PPI, expected at +0.5% on the month, and retail sales, expected at +0.3%](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ppi-retail-sales-jobless-claims-and-oil-inventories-among-economic-data-due-thursday-93CH-5633573). **Credit spreads on AI borrowers.** CoreWeave's bonds near 15% and Oracle's at 7.24% are where the selling began. If those yields keep rising while the ten-year stays near 5.20%, the problem is specific to these companies, not to rates generally. If they ease, today's equity losses rested mostly on the hike odds.
