## What changed since last report

Last night's report said the question for Wednesday was whether SpaceX and Super Micro would lift the Nasdaq above Monday's 26,430.89; futures say yes before the open, but the close decides it.

- **Revenue replaced promises as the evidence.** On Monday and Tuesday buyers paid for reported profits, such as 3M's raised forecast, and sold AI names that had only plans. Super Micro's range and SpaceX's [revenue of $14.58 billion, up 161%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/stocks-making-the-biggest-moves-premarket-spcx-smci-nflx-more.html), are reported AI revenue, which is the kind of evidence the market had been rewarding.
- **The chip selling had already gone far.** The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell eight straight sessions through October 16, [its longest run since 2008, losing about 16%](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/chip-index-sox-posts-longest-123809741.html). It closed Tuesday at 11,370.07, still about 14% below its October 6 close of 13,217.82, so a strong result meets shares that have already been marked down.
- **Rates did not help.** The 10-year was unchanged at 5.12% and Brent rose back to about $110, so this morning's gain rests on company results, not on cheaper money. [CME FedWatch shows about 85% expecting a hold on October 28 and nearly 75% a December hike](https://housingwire.com/articles/mortgage-rates-dip-treasury-yield).

## Movers

**Netflix** fell almost 7% premarket after third-quarter revenue only matched estimates and it cut its 2026 advertising forecast to $2.7 billion from $3 billion; a close below the after-hours low near $65 would show the guidance cut is still being priced.

**Intuitive Surgical** fell about 6% after its third-quarter results disappointed, a sign that not every beat-and-hold report is being rewarded this week.

## What to watch

**Whether the Nasdaq holds its premarket gain into the close.** A close above 26,430.89 would show buyers paying again for AI revenue; a fade back below Tuesday's 26,424.82, with the Dow outperforming, would show the move toward companies with reported profits still in charge. The chip index's level near its summer low around 11,000 is the floor buyers defended on Tuesday. This resolves today, before Meta and Tesla report.

**Meta's spending and Tesla's merger question tonight.** Meta has fallen five straight sessions and options imply about a 6% move; spending that grows faster than revenue would bring back the funding worry, which a CoreWeave close below Monday's 52-week low would confirm. Tesla's consensus is [about $0.43 a share, down 14% from a year earlier, on revenue of $27.79 billion](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/10/tesla-s-revenue-earnings-expected-to-edge-lower-despite-delivery-beat). [Musk said both companies have formed special committees to study a merger](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/spacex-spcx-earnings-live-updates-q3-2026html.html), and the committees have not agreed terms, so the call may draw questions on deal terms rather than margins.

**The 10-year between 5.0% and 5.331%, with oil and France pushing the same way.** Brent back above $110 and a French 10-year at 5% both argue against yields falling. A Brent settle above Tuesday's intraday high near $112 would raise the odds of a test of 5.331%; a confirmed US-Iran deal remains the clearest route below 5.0%. Thursday's jobless claims and flash PMIs, then Moody's on France on Friday, are the next tests.
