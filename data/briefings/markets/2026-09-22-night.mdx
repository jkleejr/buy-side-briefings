## Why it happened

- **This morning's read was right on the asymmetry and wrong about which channel would settle it.** It set the test as whether the Hormuz offer got a second source. It did not — and did not need to: physical barrels returned, and a three-hour meeting replaced the anonymous report.
- **The two supply stories became additive rather than alternatives.** The four-day decline through Monday priced Saudi crude rerouting *through* Hormuz at 2.9 million barrels a day; the restarted line restores the route that bypasses the strait entirely, removing most of what the $100 level was defending.
- **Yields stopped responding because the energy premium is largely out of them:** two sessions took eight basis points off the ten-year, the fifth under one. With October odds static near 60%, the long end now holds a Fed path, not a war premium.
- **The bid rotated more than it broadened.** The Nasdaq 100 reached its first record since June on a sixth straight semiconductor advance, and the Russell's 0.52% gain answered last night's breadth question — but financials fell hard enough to take the Dow down 185 points.

## What to watch

**Whether cheaper oil still reaches the long end.** Brent has fallen from roughly $108 on September 14 to $98.47, and the ten-year from 5.041% to 4.959% — but almost all of that repricing happened in two sessions, and today's decline bought nothing. A ten-year back above 5.041% with Brent still under $100 would say the long end is pricing demand rather than energy. A move below 4.90% on further crude weakness would say the transmission is still live.

**The gap between restart and full capacity.** The East-West line is flowing, but one security source briefed on the work said complete resumption could take weeks against Aramco's aim of restoring capability quickly, and seven supertankers with capacity for 14 million barrels loaded at Gulf terminals over the weekend — exports still routing through the strait. Gold fell 0.81% to $4,307.63 while the dollar index held at 100.42, so the metal is pricing some de-escalation and the currency none. Brent reclaiming $100 would say the restart is slower than Tuesday priced; holding under $95 would say the four million barrels a day are treated as back.

**The base under the record.** The Nasdaq's second record came with the Russell up 0.52%, broader than Monday, but with financials among the worst performers and semiconductors on a sixth consecutive advance carrying the index. The VIX at 14.21 is near the bottom of its recent range, so the options market is charging little either way. Flash PMIs and Powell both land Wednesday. A third record alongside a positive Russell and stabilising banks would confirm the broadening; one carried by semis alone would not.
