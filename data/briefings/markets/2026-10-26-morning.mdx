## Oil Falls Toward $110 on Iran Diplomacy, and Stock Futures Edge Up Before the Fed

Oil is giving back much of last week's gain because diplomacy with Iran picked up over the weekend, not because Saudi supply has been secured. Brent has traded between roughly $110 and $115, down from Friday's $116.72 settlement, after President Trump said there would be no US strike on Iran on Sunday and Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran. Stock futures are up about 0.3%, a small move after two losing weeks. The evidence cuts both ways: the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia have not stopped, and analysts quoted on the move describe it as traders cutting positions rather than a better supply outlook. If Brent holds below $113 into Wednesday's Fed decision, the 10-year has room to stay under 5%; a return above $116.72 would undo the morning's relief.

## What changed since last report

Sunday night's report treated Brent below $113 as the sign that the Saudi attacks were being treated as contained; most later quotes this morning are below that level, though the early Asian quotes near $114.50 were not.

- **Diplomacy, not supply.** The weekend added a no-strike statement from Trump, [Qatari mediators in Tehran, and Iran exchanging messages through intermediaries](https://www.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/10/25/trump-rules-out-iran-strike-for-today-weighs-deal-against-further-military-action). Nothing on the supply side improved: the Houthi attacks continued over the weekend.
- **Lower oil eases pressure on bonds.** The [10-year sits near 4.99% and the dollar index near 101.83](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/gold-consolidates-near-4-000-as-recovery-remains-fragile-ahead-of-fed-decision-202610261105), against Friday's 4.999% close. The move is small, which says the bond market is waiting for the Fed rather than trading the oil drop.
- **The Fed's guidance, not the decision, is what is priced to move.** With a hold at about 90%, the roughly 79% chance of a December hike is the number Wednesday's statement can change.

## What to watch

**Brent against $113 and $116.72.** Oil has dropped on talk, not on restored supply. A confirmed Iran framework or a quiet week from the Houthis would keep Brent below $113 and test the $110 area. A confirmed outage at Yanbu or on the East-West pipeline, or a collapse of the Qatari effort, would likely send it back above Friday's $116.72 settlement. Trump's "deal or finish them off" framing means this can turn either way in a single headline before the November 3 midterms.

**The 10-year against 5% into Wednesday.** The 10-year has held just under 5% while oil fell, and the 30-year near 5.30%. A Fed statement that ties further hikes to oil-driven inflation would test 5.11%, the level that would show rates being set by oil again. A softer statement that pushes back on the roughly 79% December hike pricing would likely take the 10-year back below 4.95%. The decision is due Wednesday at 2pm ET.

**AI spending into Microsoft and Alphabet on Wednesday and Amazon on Thursday.** Musk's comment adds a funding question to the neocloud selling that began this month: if smaller AI data-center operators cannot borrow, the large buyers' spending plans carry more of the demand story. A Nasdaq close above 26,424.82, about 1.8% over Friday's 25,955, would show October's tech selling ending; results that cut capital spending would test that from below. Bitcoin, at $80,351, is sitting on its $80,320 October 8 low, and a daily close below it would turn weeks of crossing that level into a break.
