## What changed since last report

Last night's report said the long end had not joined Tuesday's rally and that a thirty-year close above 5.703% would extend Monday's break. Overnight it reached that level; it has not closed there.

- **Oil reversed for a second session.** The morning decline on Tuesday rested on supply flowing around Hormuz. [The Houthis struck an Aramco facility south of Riyadh at the weekend](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/4/houthis-claim-strike-on-saudi-energy-facility-as-yemen-fighting-intensifies), adding Saudi infrastructure to the risks, and a Gulf of Mexico storm adds a US supply risk. Oil is the input that moved the long bond on Monday.
- **The rise is at the long end, not the policy end.** With October hold odds near 78%, the move is in the premium investors want for holding thirty-year debt ahead of Thursday's $22 billion sale, after Tuesday's three-year auction drew indirect bidding of 57.6% against a 65.9% average.
- **The record was narrow, so the exposure is narrow.** [Nvidia rose 2.1% on Tuesday to a record market value of about $5.76 trillion](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/trading-day-running-with-the-bulls-4935296) while the Russell 2000 fell, so the largest AI names carry most of the index's sensitivity to a higher discount rate this morning. Nasdaq futures falling about twice as far as S&P 500 futures fits that.
- **A Fed official tied AI to inflation.** Daly's remarks make chip demand a possible reason for tighter policy, not only an earnings story. If the minutes echo that, the stocks that carried Tuesday's record become a rate-sensitive group.
