The weekend added to the oil risk. ## What last week settled, and what it did not

Last week settled that the AI selling has moved from the companies buying chips to the companies making them. The semiconductor index has had two drops of more than 6% in just over a week, and Amazon recovered most of its premarket loss on Friday while Intel and AMD did not.

It did not settle whether that is about Intel or about the sector. Intel's miss was mostly its own: PC demand, supply limits and an Israel plant explosion. AMD on Tuesday is the first chipmaker to report its own numbers since.

It also did not settle what is driving bond yields. The 10-year fell about 40 bp in October as investors moved into safer assets. [December hike odds on CME FedWatch fell to 72.7% from 83.0% on Friday](https://www.actionforex.com/action-insight/market-overview/659298-dollar-falls-across-the-board-as-markets-scale-back-fed-hike-expectations-beyond-december/). Oil rose 13.6% over the same month. The two cannot both keep going, and the weekend's tanker fires add to the oil side.

## What to watch

**Oil's Sunday open.** Brent's January contract closed Friday at $113.43. The first trade shows how much of the weekend's Hormuz news the market treats as new. A 10-year back above 5.11% before Friday would mean oil's inflation risk has taken over from the demand for safety.

**AMD on Tuesday.** AMD closed at $504.80 and Nvidia at $204.64. A steady guide from AMD would make Intel's miss look specific to Intel. A cautious one would support the view that AI spending is slowing. On the Nasdaq, a close above 25,955.34 undoes last week's decline; a close under 25,437 extends it.

**Bitcoin between $76,176 and $80,320.** BTC has held above Friday's low through Saturday. A daily close under $76,176 would extend the decline. A move back above $80,320 would make the Binance theft look like a contained shock.
