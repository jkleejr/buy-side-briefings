## Chip Stocks Fell an Eighth Straight Day and Pulled the S&P 500 Below the Line It Held All Week

The chip index fell 3.8% for an eighth straight day, the longest run since 2008, and took the S&P 500 down 1.09% to 7,659.44. That is seven points below 7,666.45, the level both of the last two reports named as the sign of selling spreading beyond AI. Rates were not the cause: the two-year fell about 3 basis points and October hike odds stayed below 20%. The break was narrow: the Dow lost only 0.34% because energy rose about 2.5% with Brent near $110, while the Russell 2000 fell 1.72%. Selling reached small caps, not the whole market. A close back above 7,666.45 would point to an options-expiry overshoot; the Dow joining the decline would confirm broader selling.

## Why it happened

This morning's report said a Nasdaq that kept lagging the Dow with hike odds at 15% would show the selling was about AI financing and valuation, not rates, and that a close under 7,666.45 would show it spreading. Both happened, so the morning read held; the second half only partly, since the Dow barely fell.

- **The selling is about how AI is paid for.** [D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria told CNN it is unclear CoreWeave will earn enough to repay what it borrowed](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/16/investing/tech-stocks-ai-coreweave), and the stock is more than 60% below its 2025 peak. Huang's Thursday defense lifted the stock for one session; it did not change that question.
- **Memory and processor makers fell hardest, Nvidia least.** [Micron fell 5.6% and Intel 5.8%](https://wtop.com/national/2026/10/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10-16-2026) while [Nvidia slipped 1.17% to $214.70](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202610170540489641). Investors are selling the suppliers whose orders depend most on borrowed spending, not the main chip designer.
- **Oil kept the Dow near flat.** Energy stocks rose about 2.5% as Brent gained 3.7%, and the Dow carries far less tech than the S&P 500 or the Nasdaq. The same oil move raises the cost pressure that made a December hike plausible, which is why rates gave stocks no help.
- **Asia's rebound did not carry into the US session.** [The Kospi rose 2.05% and the Nikkei 1.39% on Friday, with SK hynix up 2.85%](https://eurasiabusinessnews.com/2026/10/16/asian-stock-markets-today-tech-rebounds-but-indonesia-plunges/), after Thursday's 7.57% and 4.9% falls. Hours later US chips fell 3.8%, so the overseas view that the selling had gone too far found no buyers in New York.
- **Monthly options expired.** Friday was the third Friday of October; the move through 7,666.45 came on that day, and a reversal next week would point to options positioning rather than new selling.

## What to watch

**The S&P 500 against 7,666.45.** The index closed seven points below it on expiry day. A close back above it early next week would show Friday as an options-driven overshoot and leave the selling confined to AI hardware. A second close below it with the Dow down more than 1% would show the selling reaching the stocks that have absorbed money all week, which has not happened yet.

**Brent against $110.** [Kpler data show about 9.5 million barrels a day leaving Hormuz in the week to Tuesday, about 30% below pre-war levels](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/16/oil-price-today-iran-strait-hormuz-ukraine-russia.html). Brent is now about $5 above the $104.7 truce line, and the diesel-first phase of the G7/IEA reserve release ends around October 22. A settle above $110 with the release tapering would raise the inflation case for a December hike that the bond market has so far set aside.

**Tesla and Meta on Wednesday.** [About 70 S&P 500 companies report next week, and 85% of the first 46 beat estimates](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/major-earnings-reports-market-fears-next-week.html). Meta's capital spending guidance is the first direct read on AI demand from a buyer since the chip selling began. Spending held or raised would argue against the slowdown chip prices now imply; a cut would support it.
