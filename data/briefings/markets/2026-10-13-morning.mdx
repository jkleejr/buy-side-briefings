## The Memory-Chip Sell-Off Spread From Seoul to Tokyo and Europe Overnight, Pulling Nasdaq Futures Lower

Monday's fall in US memory stocks went global overnight: Korea's KOSPI lost 5.46% as SK hynix fell 10% and Samsung 7.4%, and the selling reached chip-equipment makers in Tokyo and Europe. [Nasdaq 100 futures are down about 0.6% and Dow futures up about 0.2%](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-october-13-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080236234.html): a rotation out of chips, not out of stocks. The trigger is the same CXMT design claim that hit Micron on Monday, and Broadcom's chief executive added a second worry by calling capital the biggest limit on AI spending. The counter-evidence is real: [Morningstar says CXMT's design still faces major hurdles to commercialisation](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262211996-memory-stocks-selloff-sandisk-micron-drop-cxmt-new-dram-architecture-tradingkey), which suggests part of the move is positioning. If ASML and TSMC hold their guidance this week and Micron recovers $1,000, this read no longer holds.

- [The KOSPI closed down 348 points, or 5.46%, at 6,035 after briefly trading below 6,000, with a sell-side sidecar halting program selling for five minutes](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008795752). Foreign investors sold more than 4.5 trillion won, a sixth straight day of net selling, so the money leaving is foreign and persistent.
- [SK hynix fell 10.09% to 1.443 million won and Samsung 7.39% to 232,000 won](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/samsung-sk-hynix-shares-plunge-on-black-tuesday-amid-concerns-over-chinese-chip/12918371), the two companies that make most of the world's AI memory.
- [Tokyo Electron fell more than 5% and Kioxia 8.8%, while ASML opened about 3.3% lower and STMicro and Infineon about 5% lower](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Equipment and analog names falling with memory means the selling is no longer confined to the companies CXMT competes with.
- [Broadcom's Hock Tan said capital "might be the biggest constraint" on AI growth, and Intel's Lip-Bu Tan made a similar point](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/12/intel-broadcom-ceos-warn-capital-crunch-could-become-ai-bottleneck.html). Tan also said demand for compute still exceeds supply, so the warning is about who pays for the build-out, not about falling demand.
- [Micron was down about 3% before the US open, with SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital also about 3% lower](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/sk-hynix-softbank-samsung-electronics-shares-tech-stocks-fall.html). Micron is trading under $1,000 for the first time since September.

## What changed since last report

Last night's report said chips were the equity risk and that steady TSMC guidance would keep the selling inside memory; the overnight session has widened it to equipment before TSMC reports.

- **Foreign money left Korea in size.** A sixth day of foreign net selling, above 4.5 trillion won on Tuesday alone, turned Monday's 3.65% KOSPI loss into a 5.46% one. Korea is the most direct holding of the AI memory boom, so its outflows lead the US names.
- **A capital warning reached the same investors.** Hock Tan's remark landed on a sector already falling, and it questions the spending that ASML, Tokyo Electron and Nvidia depend on, which is why equipment fell alongside memory.
- **Bonds moved the other way.** [The ten-year fell more than 5 basis points to 5.1937% as oil eased](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/us-treasurys-bonds-yields.html), so the chip selling is not a rates story; lower yields are supporting banks and the Dow.
- **Oil gave back part of Monday's gain** but Brent remains above the $104.7 line set yesterday, so the market still does not trust the energy truce, which [Trump insists is in place while a Russian drone killed a woman in Kyiv](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/13/trump-insists-russia-ukraine-energy-ceasefire-is-in-place-drone-strike-kills-woman-in-kyiv).
