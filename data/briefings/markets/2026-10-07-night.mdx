## Stocks Slipped From Their Records as Yields Touched New 2002 Highs, Then a Strong Ten-Year Sale Pulled Them Back

The ten-year yield reached about 5.365% and the thirty-year 5.732% before 1 p.m., both the highest since 2002. Then the $39 billion ten-year auction drew the strongest foreign-type demand in months, and yields fell back. The September minutes an hour later added little: most officials expected another hike this year, with no date. The S&P 500 closed down 0.22% at 7,801.77, while the Russell 2000 fell 1.31%. Buyers turned up at 5.30%, which weakens the idea that the long end is rising because demand is scarce. It does not explain why yields are this high, and with oil near $100 and a Fed leaning toward a hike, that stays open. A thirty-year close above 5.732% would show the auction did not cap yields.

- [The ten-year sold near 5.30% with indirect bidders taking 80.3% against a 72.4% ten-auction average, after the yield touched about 5.365% intraday, the highest since April 2002](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/treasury-yields-auction-fomc-minutes.html). The auction showed demand at these yields, not scarcity.
- [The thirty-year reached 5.732%, its highest since May 2002](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/treasury-yields-auction-fomc-minutes.html), and ended near 5.67-5.68%, above the 5.644% 2002 high and below Monday's 5.703%.
- [The minutes of the September 15-16 meeting showed most officials saw another increase as likely appropriate before year-end, with no timing](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). They confirmed the bias the market already prices, and [October hike odds stayed near 20%](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html).
- [The S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,801.77, the Nasdaq 0.22% to 27,538.69 and the Dow 0.66% to 51,179.87, while the Russell 2000 fell 1.31% to 2,793.36](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-oct-7-133458375.html). The decline was largest in the smallest companies, which depend more on borrowing costs.
- [Brent settled down 38 cents at $100.20 and WTI down $1.16 at $88.28 after the IEA agreed to prioritise releasing diesel stocks](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/07/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz.html), reversing early gains on new Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia.

## Why it happened

This morning's report was right that yields would test their highs and stocks would open lower: both yields set new 2002 highs and the S&P 500 fell. Its two drivers held up less well. The auction it said would favour the supply explanation went the other way, and oil settled lower instead of extending its rise.

- **The rise before 1 p.m. came with oil.** Brent traded above $101 on [Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/07/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz.html), and yields peaked going into the auction. The ten-to-thirty-year gap held near 37 basis points, close to Tuesday's 39, so the selling was spread along the curve rather than concentrated at the long end.
- **The auction removed one explanation.** 80.3% indirect take-up, about 8 points above average, is the opposite of Tuesday's three-year sale at 57.6%. Foreign and real-money buyers came in at 5.30%, so the long end is not rising because nobody will buy. What remains is investors asking more to hold long debt with inflation above target, which an auction cannot fix.
- **The minutes added nothing new.** Officials leaning toward a hike was already priced, so neither yields nor stocks moved much at 2 p.m. Rate-sensitive stocks still fell: [Goldman Sachs and Bank of America each fell about 1%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) six days before bank results.
- **Oil reversed on supply, not demand.** The [EIA reported a 3.2 million-barrel crude draw against expectations of a build](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/weekly/), normally a reason for higher prices. The IEA's decision to release diesel first outweighed it, though much of the roughly 100 million barrels was already pledged.
