## Oil Rose to Its Highest Since Mid-September as the Russia-Ukraine Energy Truce Faltered, While Stocks Held Near Their Record

Brent settled up 1.8% at $106.65, its highest close since September 15, a day after President Trump announced an energy ceasefire that Moscow has not confirmed and Kyiv says Russia has already broken. That reverses the morning's move: oil traded near $103 early and closed above Friday's $104.7 settlement, the level this morning's report set for the market no longer trusting the truce. The S&P 500 slipped only 0.06%, so equities absorbed the higher oil price. The evidence cuts both ways: Gulf flows are still moving, but Wednesday's CPI is the first print with a full month of Brent above $100. If Brent falls back under $104.7 on a confirmed truce, this read no longer holds.

## Why it happened

This morning's report was wrong on oil, which reversed through its own $104.7 line, and right that chips were the equity risk, with the S&P 500 held under its record by semiconductors.

- **The truce lost credibility within hours.** Zelensky's charge of violations and the Kremlin's refusal to confirm the deal removed the case for more Russian supply, which was the whole basis of the early drop.
- **Memory makers fell on a Chinese design, not on demand.** [CXMT said a new cell architecture can raise memory density 30-50%, with a server product due by the end of 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/micron-stock-drops-china-cxmt-173326080.html). That is a pricing threat to Micron and SK hynix, and it followed Samsung's 4.4% fall in Seoul.
- **Gold slipped as the dollar firmed.** [Spot gold fell about $24 to $4,169.50 by 4:38 pm](https://www.kitco.com/charts/gold), and [USAGOLD cited a firm dollar and high yields capping the metal in thin holiday trade](https://www.usagold.com/daily-precious-metals-market-report-october-12-2026/). Gold fell while oil rose, so the oil move reads as a supply worry rather than demand for a hedge.
- **Stocks absorbed oil because the money rotated rather than left.** [Nine of eleven S&P 500 sectors rose, with energy leading](https://investrade.com/market-review-october-12-2026/), while technology lagged on the chip selling. An index flat on the day with breadth that positive says investors are trimming crowded chip holdings, not cutting risk, ahead of a season where [S&P 500 third-quarter earnings are tracking 29.6% growth](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/earnings-season-kicks-into-high-gear-as-big-banks-report-next-week.html).
- **No signal from rates.** Cash Treasuries were shut, so Tuesday's open is the first test of whether $106 oil pushes the ten-year back toward 5.331%.

## What to watch

**Brent against the truce.** Brent is $1.95 above the $104.7 line and at its highest since September 15. A confirmation from Moscow and a halt to grid and refinery strikes would put it back under $104.7. Further strikes or a Houthi hit that stops Saudi loadings would take it toward the September highs. The IEA board meets Wednesday and Thursday to set the diesel-first reserve release, the first supply response on the calendar.

**CPI against 5.24%.** The ten-year reopens Tuesday near 5.24%, 9 basis points under the 5.331% high, with Brent about $2 higher than when bonds last traded. [CME FedWatch put December hike odds above 75% on October 2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/fed-rate-hike-odds-decline-after-september-jobs-report.html). The same preview expects core to rise 0.2% on the month, so the oil effect should show in the headline figure; a core monthly gain of 0.3% or more on Wednesday would likely take the ten-year through 5.331%; 0.2% or less would ease the pressure on stocks near their record.

**Chips into ASML and TSMC.** The semiconductor index has fallen for several sessions, and Nvidia closed about 5% under its $239.24 October 6 record. ASML on Wednesday and TSMC on Thursday decide whether the selling is about memory competition or about AI spending. Seoul is the early signal: [the KOSPI fell 3.65% on Monday](https://koreatimes.co.kr/economy/others/20261012/kospi-plunges-365-despite-samsungs-record-earnings) on foreign selling of Samsung and SK hynix, and Tuesday's session there ends before the US open. Steady TSMC guidance would confine the selling to memory; an S&P 500 close under 7,666.45 would show it spreading.
