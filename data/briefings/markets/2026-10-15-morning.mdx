## What changed since last report

Last night's report read Wednesday's fall as a rates story and named TSMC guidance as the test for chips. TSMC passed that test, but chip stocks kept falling, and the rates side eased. That read now covers less of what is happening.

- **Fed officials pushed back on an October hike.** [The dollar retreated from an 18-month high as Fed officials tempered hike bets](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/dollar-falls-from-18-month-high-despite-hot-cpi-as-fed-curb-hike-bets.html). Part of the fall in yields is that pushback, and part is investors buying Treasuries while they sell stocks.
- **TSMC's outlook was strong on demand but cautious on margins.** Fourth-quarter gross margin guidance of 65-67% sits against 66.4% just reported, and [capital spending stays at $60-64 billion](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/tsmc-q3-2026-slides-ai-boom-drives-record-revenue-despite-margin-pressure-93CH-4950708). Unchanged spending gave equipment makers nothing new.
- **In Tokyo the selling was narrow, not broad.** [About 70% of Nikkei stocks rose even as the index lost 1,253 points](https://finance.biggo.com/news/7fd77533-71f1-41ab-b7e3-830e43f1c6dc), with Advantest, SoftBank Group and Tokyo Electron accounting for roughly 1,200 of them. That fits investors selling AI exposure and keeping the rest of their holdings, which is the same pattern the Dow's 0.09% loss showed in New York on Wednesday.
- **Memory makers are falling on Chinese competition as well as funding worries.** CXMT's push into memory has been the reason given for this selling since Tuesday, and SK hynix's 11% loss is larger than anything the rate move explains.

## What to watch

**PPI and retail sales at 8:30.** [The September PPI is due at 8:30](https://www.bls.gov/ppi/), with consensus near +0.5% on the month and +0.3% core, and retail sales near +0.3%. A hot PPI would pull the two-year back toward 4.81% and October hike odds back above 40%. If tech still falls on a soft PPI, today's selling is not about rates. Jobless claims, expected near 200,000, would need to rise well above that before the labour market gave the Fed a reason to wait.

**The S&P 500 against 7,666.45.** Futures down about 0.3% put the index near 7,695, about 29 points above the level. The Dow is rising and the Nasdaq falling, so the selling is still a rotation within the market rather than a fall across it. A close under 7,666.45 would mean that rotation has broken down.

**Credit for AI borrowers against Treasuries.** CoreWeave's and Oracle's bond yields hit records while the ten-year fell to 5.10%. If that gap keeps widening, the problem is these companies' borrowing, and lower rates will not fix it. If CoreWeave's bonds steady while its shares keep falling, the selling is about valuation rather than solvency. Fed Chair Warsh's remarks on Friday are the last Fed guidance before the October 28 meeting.
