## What changed since last report

The synchronization is the information. One country repricing its long end is a fiscal story; four doing it at once, with Japan and the UK at levels last seen in 1996 and 1998, is the market re-rating the price of duration itself. Two inputs are stacking rather than alternating. The barrel supplies the inflation impulse — Brent has held above $94 through the escalation — and it argues for tighter policy everywhere at once, which is why gilts and Bunds moved on a US-Iran headline. Underneath sits supply: hyperscalers have raised roughly $132 billion this year against $109 billion in all of 2025, almost all long-dated, landing in the maturity buckets sovereigns are trying to fund. The tell that this is inflation and supply rather than fear held again — gold fell 1.86% Tuesday and the dollar barely moved while US drones hit Iranian tankers. Equities take it through the multiple, not the earnings line, which is why the Nasdaq leads lower.

Tuesday's report named 7,650 as the level marking equities adjusting, and the index closed below it at 7,631.47. It flagged the JGB at 3% in its table but framed the read around the barrel; overnight put the global leg at the centre. Confirmation is still missing: the 30-year has traded above 5.30% twice and closed there neither time. Base case (55%): the 10-year holds 4.75–4.90%, the 30-year stays under a 5.30% close, the S&P ranges 7,580–7,700 into Friday. Bull (20%): a soft ADP pulls futures toward the prediction-market read, the 10-year returns under 4.70%, the S&P reclaims 7,700. Bear (25%): a 30-year close above 5.30%, or Brent through $100 on a measured export disruption, and the S&P loses 7,550.
