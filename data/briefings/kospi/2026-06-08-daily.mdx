## 📅 What Changed Since the Last Briefing

**Korea is decoupling — but only at the stock level, not the index level.** The story of the week is rotation: the same AI capital that [observers blame for draining bitcoin](https://en.yna.co.kr/) is the lifeblood of Korean memory makers. SK Hynix and, increasingly, Samsung are levered to HBM, the memory that AI accelerators cannot run without. That is why the KOSPI fell less than the S&P 500 on Friday and why SK Hynix can trade green while almost everything else is red.

**But the index is hostage to the won.** [As the dollar firms on higher-for-longer US rates, USD/KRW is grinding toward 1,400](https://www.koreaherald.com/) — a level that matters psychologically and mechanically. A weak won eats the foreign-currency returns of overseas investors, and when those investors are already [net sellers](https://www.reuters.com/markets/asia/), a break of 1,400 can become self-reinforcing: outflows weaken the won, the weaker won prompts more outflows.

**Oil is the under-appreciated headwind.** Korea imports nearly all of its energy. The [US-Iran escalation near Hormuz](https://www.koreaherald.com/) that is keeping a bid under oil flows straight through to Korea's trade balance and inflation — and it complicates any Bank of Korea support for the won. The base case into the week is a resilient-but-capped KOSPI: the AI-memory bid holds the index up, the FX and macro headwinds cap the upside, and the resolution waits on US CPI.

## 🪞 Bear Case (counter-argument)

Korea is a high-beta, FX-sensitive, export-driven market, and it faces a triple headwind: a strong dollar dragging the won toward 1,400, a global risk-off led by a hot US labor market and [30Y yields above 5.0%](https://www.koreaherald.com/), and an [oil spike from the US-Iran conflict](https://www.koreaherald.com/) that taxes an energy importer directly. The semiconductor concentration that is the index's tailwind today is also its single biggest risk — a third of the KOSPI rides on one AI-memory narrative, and if HBM pricing or Nvidia's capex blinks, the relative-strength leader becomes the relative-weakness epicenter. [Foreign investors, already net sellers](https://www.reuters.com/markets/asia/), set the marginal price; a decisive break above 1,400 USD/KRW typically accelerates outflows in a self-reinforcing loop.

**Invalidation level:** a hot May US CPI on June 10 that drives USD/KRW through 1,400 and the KOSPI below 3,150 turns this HOLD into a STEP ASIDE on the whole market; conversely, a cool CPI with the won holding below 1,400 and foreign buyers returning reopens a broad-index long.
